# v1.11 — Dr. M., Iteration 7: BNOSSW MMI prefactor sensitivity

**Date:** 2026-05-08
**Engine version:** v1.11.0
**New module:** `src/itb/prefactor_sensitivity.py` — sweep a constraint's prefactor across frameworks to find the transition value at which each framework's status flips.

---

## What Dr. M. did

I'd noticed that across 6 previous iterations, none of my new constraints had flipped any framework's status (LQG's persistent BNOSSW MMI failure aside). Before adding more constraints, I wanted to know whether the existing key finding — LQG-induced fails BNOSSW MMI — was *robust* to the prefactor choice or sat on a knife-edge that publication-grade encoding could erase.

I built a `PrefactorSensitivity` analysis tool and applied it to BNOSSW MMI:

```
prefactor * g_4*g_6/(g_4+g_6) >= g_R2
```

Sweep prefactor from 0.5 to 2.5 in 21 steps. Canonical BNOSSW prefactor: 1.0.

## What the engine found

| framework | transition prefactor | interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Pure GR | never (always vacuous) | trivially satisfied at origin |
| String tree EFT | **0.900** | passes canonical with 10% margin |
| Asymptotic Safety | **0.900** | passes canonical with 10% margin |
| **CDT** | **1.000** | **knife-edge at canonical value** |
| LQG-induced | **1.200** | needs 20% looser bound to pass |

## Why this is genuinely informative

**Three new findings that weren't visible from any prior iteration:**

### Finding 1 — LQG's MMI failure is robust, not artifactual

The transition is at prefactor 1.20 — meaning the bound has to be 20% looser than canonical for LQG to pass. This is a wide margin. **Whatever the literal published BNOSSW prefactor turns out to be (likely close to 1.0 but could be 0.9 or 1.1), LQG-induced will still fail it.** The failure is structurally robust.

### Finding 2 — CDT is on a knife-edge

This is *new* this iteration. Previous reports treated CDT as comfortably feasible. The sensitivity analysis reveals that CDT sits exactly at MMI's canonical value — passes at prefactor ≥ 1.0, fails at prefactor < 1.0. **A publication-grade BNOSSW MMI encoding for n=3 regions could flip CDT from feasible to infeasible** depending on the precise prefactor. This is the first time the engine has flagged CDT as marginal.

### Finding 3 — String-EFT and AS have a 10% safety margin

Both transition at prefactor 0.9. They are robustly feasible — even a 10% tighter MMI form would not exclude them. Among the parity-conserving frameworks, string-EFT and AS are the *most* robust to MMI prefactor choice; CDT is the *least* robust.

## What this changes about Dr. M.'s recommendation

In iteration 6 I recommended replacing toy prefactors with publication values. Now I have *quantitative* evidence that this matters:

- String, AS: prefactor sensitivity is low; publication-grade encoding will not change their status.
- LQG: failure is robust; will survive publication-grade encoding.
- **CDT: prefactor sensitivity is high; status uncertain under publication-grade encoding.**

The next research-grade encoding pass should prioritize getting BNOSSW MMI's prefactor right — that single number determines whether CDT is in or out of the engine's allowed region.

## Methodologically what's new

This is the engine's first use of *prefactor sensitivity sweeps* as a meta-analysis. Up to now, every analysis tool produced a single answer for a single constraint configuration. Sweeping the prefactor and looking for *transitions* is structurally different — it's testing the engine's findings for robustness rather than just computing them.

This generalizes: I could run prefactor sweeps on every constraint with an O(1) parameter and produce a "robustness map" for the engine's framework-discrimination findings. Each entry: "framework X's status with respect to constraint Y is robust within prefactor range [a, b]." That would be a publication-actionable artifact — telling the field which findings are knife-edge and which are robust.
