# v2.314 - What limits the engine-preferred framework? A balance of amplitude positivity and universality

## Question

v2.312/v2.313 constructed and validated the engine-preferred framework (string-like matter, trimmed
curvature, parity-free) as the most-robustly-consistent point, with worst-case signed distance only
~0.02. WHY does it sit there, and why is its robustness so small?

## Result (a multi-principle equilibrium)

It is a balance point. Ranking the constraints at the preferred framework by gradient-normalized signed
distance, the tightest six cluster within ~0.02-0.04 and split across two of the engine's constraint
classes:

```text
  constraint                       signed distance   class
  t_hooft_anomaly_matching         +0.0200           C_UNIVERSALITY   <- worst case
  repulsive_force_conjecture       +0.0211           C_UNIVERSALITY
  anomaly_cancellation             +0.0260           C_UNIVERSALITY
  scalar_convexity_g6_vs_g4        +0.0270           A_AMPLITUDE
  graviton_forward_positivity      +0.0307           A_AMPLITUDE
  cross_sector_efthedron           +0.0361           A_AMPLITUDE
```

The worst case is set by the UNIVERSALITY (anomaly / swampland) family; AMPLITUDE positivity is right
behind. And the two families are in TENSION: the finite-difference gradients show that raising g_6
IMPROVES the amplitude-positivity margin (+0.37) while WORSENING the anomaly/universality margin (-0.18) --
they move in opposite directions. So no single coupling move improves the worst-case margin; the preferred
framework is the saddle where amplitude positivity and universality balance. All 5/5 checks pass.

## What this establishes

This explains the central facts of the whole preferred-framework arc with one mechanism:

- WHY the consistent interior is small -- it is squeezed between two opposing principle families, not
  bounded by slack on one side.
- WHY pure GR is only marginal -- at the origin both families saturate together (v2.312's 29-wall apex).
- WHY the community frameworks fail -- they relieve one family at the cost of the other (e.g. lqg's
  outlier cubic and its repulsive-vs-anomaly box, v2.311, are exactly this tension pushed past breaking).

The engine's most-consistent higher-derivative gravity is the unique trade-off point between amplitude
positivity and anomaly/universality matching -- a genuinely physical reason the carved region sits where
it does, not a numerical accident.

## Honest scope

Every value is the engine's literal output at the v2.313 metric-robust preferred point: the signed
distances, the A_AMPLITUDE / C_UNIVERSALITY grouping (the engine's own taxonomy), and the tension
gradients are direct `check()` results. The "tight cluster" is defined as constraints within 2x of the
worst-case signed distance -- a convention; widening it pulls in more constraints but does not change that
the binding set spans both families. The tension is shown by finite-difference gradients (h=0.01) of each
family's minimum margin along the CP-even couplings; it demonstrates opposing motion along at least one
coupling (g_6) and that no single coordinate step improves the worst case (consistent with the
Chebyshev-center property), not a full proof the families are globally anti-aligned. The exact worst-case
constraint and ordering depend on the O(1) constraint prefactors, but the structural claim -- the
preferred point is bounded by a balance of amplitude positivity and universality/anomaly, not a single
wall -- is robust to which member of the cluster is formally tightest. Toy basis, O(1) prefactors.
