## Question

v2.17 showed that continuous tower/species surrogates pass a basic sanity gate: they
reconnect the parity lobes, avoid near-zero parity punctures, and preserve known
framework verdicts. The next question was whether they add an independent constraint wall.

This audit samples a targeted mixture of:

- v2.13 lobe centroids,
- parity-zero bridge witnesses,
- lobe-to-lobe segments with local jitter,
- broad stress points in the same 8D box.

For each tower surrogate at `N_max=3.0`, it measures:

- sampled island growth when the surrogate is removed,
- how many points are admitted by the corrected stack without the old distance/species
  sector but rejected by the surrogate,
- overlap of surrogate failures with the legacy `swampland_distance_conjecture`,
  `species_scale_bound`, and `complexity_cutoff`.

This is a targeted overlap audit, not a global Monte Carlo volume theorem.

## Interpretation

This is a negative independence result.

The v2.17 tower surrogates are cleaner than the hard max/min distance prior because they
are continuous through parity zero and carry an explicit species/tower interpretation.
But at the permissive cap `N_max=3.0`, they do not add a sampled wall in the corrected
v2.13 lobe/bridge region. When they fail broad stress points, they overwhelmingly overlap
the existing species-scale and complexity-cutoff aggregate curvature sector.

So the current evidence is:

1. The hard distance prior is bad for topology.
2. Continuous tower surrogates pass sanity gates.
3. Those surrogates are still redundant in this basis at `N_max=3.0`.

The missing physical ingredient is not another aggregate norm. It is a resolved
moduli/tower model: a field-space coordinate, a tower mass law, and a species cutoff that
is not just another curvature-load cap.
