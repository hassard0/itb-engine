## The intuition — and why it fails

All four ingested experiments are weak-field. Neutron stars are the strongest
**gravity** accessible (compactness C = GM/Rc² ≈ 0.2), so the natural guess is that
NS tidal deformability — measured by GW170817 — is the *sharpest* probe of
higher-derivative curvature corrections. **It is the opposite: the weakest.**

## The scaling

A term g R²/M² corrects the tidal deformability at the relative level

        δΛ/Λ ~ g_curv · (E_curv / M_cutoff)² ,    E_curv ~ ℏc / R_NS .

The crucial distinction (Dr. M.-confirmed): **strong gravity ≠ high curvature-energy.**
The compactness is O(0.2), but the curvature in *energy* units is

        E_curv ~ ℏc / (12 km) ≈ **1.6×10⁻¹¹ eV** ,

far below a meV cutoff. So at the dark-energy cutoff, δΛ/Λ ≈ (1.6×10⁻¹¹/2.4×10⁻³)² ≈
**1.9×10⁻¹⁷** — utterly invisible (GW170817 measures Λ̃ to ~tens of percent, needing
δΛ/Λ ≳ 0.1).

## Result — NS tides are the weakest curvature-cutoff probe

| probe | sector | highest cutoff reached |
|---|---|---|
| sub-mm gravity | matter (light scalaron) | **2.4 meV** (directly excites the scalaron) |
| GW dispersion | tensor (lever arm) | **1.8 meV** (cumulative phase, v1.85) |
| GW speed | tensor (arrival time) | 1.2×10⁻⁵ eV (v1.84) |
| **NS tides** | strong gravity (static) | **3.3×10⁻¹¹ eV** |

With Λ measured to ~10% and **no propagation lever arm**, NS tides reach only
~10⁻¹¹ eV — **blind to the meV/dark-energy cutoff, and weaker than even the GW speed
test.** The strongest-gravity regime is the *least* sensitive curvature-cutoff probe.

## The honest scalar-vs-tensor distinction (Dr. M.)

- The **tensor** higher-derivative correction (R², C²) is blind, because E_curv is low.
- The light dark-energy **scalaron** (matter fifth force) *could* affect Λ — but it is
  **screened** (chameleon/Vainshtein) in the dense NS interior, leaving the star
  GR-like. So NS tides are *blind to the tensor sector and screened from the scalar
  sector* — doubly insensitive to the dark-energy-scale physics.

## The lesson (extending v1.84–85)

This sharpens the program's recurring theme: **which observable, and at what energy
and precision, decides what a sector can probe — not how "strong" the field looks.**
Reaching the dark-energy cutoff requires either directly exciting a light mode
(sub-mm) or a huge cumulative lever arm (GW dispersion). Neutron stars, despite the
strongest gravity, have neither — low curvature-energy and a static, ~10%-precision
observable.

## Honest scope

Order-of-magnitude (no modified-TOV solve); the robust content is the **scaling** and
the **compactness-vs-curvature-energy** distinction, both Dr. M.-confirmed. δΛ at the
dark-energy cutoff is ~10⁻¹⁷ for every framework (g_curv ~ 0.1–1.3) — none observable.

**Neutron-star tides are blind to the dark-energy-cutoff curvature sector** — the
weakest of all the program's curvature probes — because strong gravity is not high
