# v2.254 - The Weak Gravity Conjecture: 'gravity is the weakest force' as a QG-consistency test

## Question

A fresh QG-CONSISTENCY (swampland) probe, reconnecting to the engine's `distance_conjecture` /
`eft_validity` sector. Quantum gravity is conjectured to forbid stable extremal charged black-hole
remnants: an extremal Reissner-Nordstrom hole has `Q = M` (Planck units), and for it to DISCHARGE
there must exist a particle with `q/m >=` the extremal ratio. Does the Standard Model satisfy this,
and by how much?

## Result

The super-extremality parameter `z = q g M_Pl / m` (with `g = sqrt(4 pi alpha) ~ 0.303`) must be
`>= 1`; the force ratio is `F_gauge/F_grav = z^2`:

```text
  particle    q/m (Planck)    force ratio (q/m)^2    WGC (>=1)?
  electron    7.2e21          5.2e43                 YES
  muon        3.5e19          1.2e39                 YES
  tau         2.1e18          4.3e36                 YES
  up quark    1.7e21          2.8e42                 YES
  proton      3.9e18          1.6e37                 YES
```

**Every Standard-Model charged particle satisfies the WGC by enormous margins.** The electron's
electromagnetic force exceeds its gravity by `~5e43` (43 orders of magnitude) -- "gravity is the
weakest force" by a vast margin -- and even the heaviest charged particle here, the proton, has
`z ~ 4e18`. So the SM sits deep in the WGC-allowed region, far from the swampland boundary `z = 1`
where an EFT with no super-extremal state would be UV-incompletable in quantum gravity. The
complementary magnetic WGC bounds the EFT cutoff `Lambda <~ g M_Pl ~ 3.7e27 eV` (near Planck, the
monopole scale).

This is a QG-CONSISTENCY probe (does the low-energy theory admit a quantum-gravity completion?),
complementing the session's QG-PHENOMENOLOGY probes (what does QG predict observably?), and it ties
to the engine's `distance_conjecture` / `eft_validity` swampland sector -- the constraint program's
"is this theory in the landscape or the swampland?" axis.
