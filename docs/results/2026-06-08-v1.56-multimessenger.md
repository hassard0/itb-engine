# v1.56 — Multimessenger parity: the GW+EM birefringence test (and why the naive ratio isn't clean)

**Date:** 2026-06-08
**Compute:** Vulcan.
**Probe:** multimessenger (single high-z GW+EM source).

---

## The idea, and an honest test of it

If one dark-energy axion (v1.46) sources both gravitational parity (g_R2_parity,
Chern–Simons R R̃) and photon parity (axion–photon F F̃), a single high-z source
emitting both GW and EM should show *both* polarizations rotating as they cross
the same axion field history. The hope: the ratio β_GW/β_EM cancels the
absolute-normalization uncertainty (both share the path) and gives a clean test
of the common origin.

## Finding 1 — source must be high-z (the axion rolls late)

The thawing axion rolls only once dark energy dominates (z ≲ 0.7), so the
birefringence accumulates at low redshift:

| source z | field-roll fraction along path | β_EM |
|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 0.22 | 0.08° |
| 0.10 | 0.40 | 0.14° |
| 0.50 | 0.97 | 0.33° |
| ≥ 1.0 | 1.00 | 0.34° |

**GW170817 (z = 0.0099) captures only ~5% of the effect** — far too nearby. A
multimessenger parity test needs **z ≳ 0.5–1** for either messenger to accumulate
a detectable rotation.

## Finding 2 (honest) — the ratio is NOT a clean cancellation

The two effects are structurally different observables:

- **EM birefringence**: a *frequency-independent* (achromatic) polarization-**angle**
  rotation, β_EM = ½ g_φγ Δφ, with g_φγ = (α/2π)(c_γ/f) — it carries the EM
  fine-structure constant α.
- **GW birefringence** (gravitational Chern–Simons): an *amplitude asymmetry*
  between L/R circular GW modes that is *frequency-dependent* and grows with the
  GW wavenumber; no α factor.

So β_GW/β_EM ~ (c_grav/c_γ)(2π/α) × (frequency-dependent structural factor) — it
depends on the GW frequency and the coupling structure and does **not** cleanly
reduce to a pure anomaly-coefficient ratio. The "distance cancels" argument holds
for the shared *path*, but the *observables* are an angle vs an
amplitude-asymmetry. The clean-ratio premise fails.

## What the real multimessenger test is

**Joint parity detection from one z ≳ 0.5 source:** both messengers show parity
violation tracking the *same* axion field history Δφ(z) along the line of sight.
The correlation — both present, both scaling with the same roll — confirms the
common dark-energy-axion origin, even though the precise ratio is structural
rather than a clean constant. Experiment: next-generation GW detectors
(Einstein Telescope / Cosmic Explorer, which reach high z) plus GRB/kilonova
polarimetry of high-z multimessenger events.

## Status

This refines (and partly walks back) the clean-ratio hope: the multimessenger
handle is a *correlation/joint-detection* test, not a normalization-canceling
ratio, and it requires high-z sources. It remains a genuine cross-check of the
common-origin claim, complementary to the single-sector probes (GW birefringence,
chiral Hellings–Downs, CMB EB) — but it is not the clean discriminator first
imagined. Honest result; the structural difference between EM-angle and
GW-amplitude birefringence is the lesson.
