# v1.52 — The engine as a live inference machine: which theory does the data favour?

**Date:** 2026-06-08
**Compute:** Vulcan.
**New capability:** `src/itb/inference.py` (Bayesian framework posterior).

---

## What this adds

A new engine layer turns the question around: not "which theories are
consistent" but **"which theories does a measurement favour."** Given
measurements of Wilson coefficients (or observables that map to them) with
Gaussian uncertainties, `framework_posterior()` returns the posterior over the
candidate theories (5 tests; 379 suite green).

## Demonstration — the posterior reshaping as data arrives

Posterior over the 8 candidate theories (uniform prior) under a sequence of
measurement scenarios:

| scenario | top survivors |
|---|---|
| 1. prior (no data) | all 8 at 12.5% |
| 2. + GW birefringence **null** (g_R2_parity = 0 ± 0.05) | parity-conserving ~16% each; **LQG → 4.5%, parity-branch → 3%** |
| 3. + sub-mm Yukawa λ≈90 µm (g_R2 = 0.20 ± 0.03) | **string 34%, CDT 28%, high-g8 26%**; AS 9%, novel 3% |
| 4. *instead*: GW birefringence **detected** (g_R2_parity = +0.09 ± 0.02) | **LQG 98%** |
| 5. + matter scattering (g_8 = 0.05 ± 0.03) | **discovered_novel 78%**, Pure GR 22% |

Each measurement carves the theory space differently, and the engine reports
exactly how. Three features worth noting:

- **A GW-birefringence null** (scenario 2) immediately disfavours the
  parity-violating candidates (LQG, the discovered branch) and lifts the
  parity-conserving ones — the cleanest near-term discriminator at work.
- **Handedness sensitivity** (scenario 4): a *positive* g_R2_parity = +0.09
  favours LQG (+0.08), while a *negative* −0.09 would favour the discovered
  parity branch (−0.092). Parity violation carries a sign, and the inference
  distinguishes the handedness — a physically correct, automatically-emergent
  feature.
- **Complementarity:** sub-mm gravity (g_R2) and matter scattering (g_8) pick
  among the parity-conserving survivors that birefringence cannot separate.

## Why it matters

This makes the engine *operational*: as real results land (DESI, LiteBIRD,
LIGO O5, torsion-balance updates), feed them in and read off the surviving
theory space — no re-derivation. It is the natural home for the prediction
scorecard (v1.51): the scorecard says what each experiment tests; this layer
computes what each *result* implies.

## Honest limitations

Gaussian likelihoods, uniform prior, framework point-values (no per-framework
coefficient uncertainty yet — that would widen the posteriors, the natural next
upgrade). Maps observables to single Wilson coefficients; multi-coefficient
observables (the matter forward amplitude) are entered as their dominant
coefficient.
