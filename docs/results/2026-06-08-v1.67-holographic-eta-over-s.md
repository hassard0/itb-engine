## What this adds

A new holographic observable in `gravitational_observables.py`,
`HolographicEtaOverS` — the shear-viscosity-to-entropy ratio of a putative
AdS/CFT dual. For Gauss-Bonnet / R² gravity (Dr. M.-confirmed standard form,
Brigante–Liu–Myers–Shenker–Yaida):

    η/s = (1/4π)(1 − 8 λ_GB),   λ_GB = lam_map · g_R2

so a positive leading curvature coupling g_R2 **lowers η/s below the KSS bound
1/4π**. We report η/s in units of 1/4π; values < 1 mean the dual violates KSS.
Wired into the `itb predict` fingerprint; 4 tests; suite **410 green**.

## Two findings

1. **Every framework's dual violates the KSS bound** (η/s < 1), by 24–70%,
   because they all have positive leading curvature coupling g_R2. The
   parity-violating branch (largest g_R2 = 0.40) sits at η/s = 0.305 — **right at
   the causality floor (0.28)**: the engine's most distinctive new-physics
   candidate is also the most extreme holographically, nearly saturating the
   causality bound on Gauss-Bonnet coupling.

2. **η/s is an ORTHOGONAL discriminator.** It orders frameworks by the *leading*
   curvature coupling g_R2 — a different axis from the cubic-suppression (g_R3)
   ordering that the constraints (forward positivity, EFThedron, cubic-matter) all
   use. So η/s separates frameworks the constraint-based survival does not: e.g.
   LQG (0.47) vs the parity branch (0.31) are both "large g_R2" but distinguished;
   AS (0.74) and discovered_novel (0.76) are the holographically gentlest. The
   information-theoretic sector adds a genuinely independent handle to the
   fingerprint.

## Honest limitations (Dr. M.'s flag)

The toy g_R2 (~0.2–0.4) **cannot be λ_GB directly** — that would give η/s < 0
(unphysical) and blow through causality. `lam_map ≈ 0.22` maps g_R2 to λ_GB so the
largest framework g_R2 sits just under the causality bound; this mapping is
order-of-magnitude. The robust content is the **ordering by g_R2** and the
qualitative facts: all duals violate KSS, the parity branch is near the causality
floor, and η/s is orthogonal to the cubic-suppression ordering. The observable
also assumes the framework admits a holographic dual (not all do) — it is a "what
would the dual's viscosity be" prediction.
