# v2.01 — Bayesian model comparison: which quantum-gravity framework does the data most favor?

**Date:** 2026-06-09
**Compute:** Vulcan.

---

## The question

The whole catalogue of 14 frameworks, one posterior. Given the ingested data, **which
quantum gravity does the data most favor**, and by what Bayes factor? We marginalize each
framework's likelihood over a small prior ball, with the **theoretical stack as the prior**
(a framework's weight is the fraction of its ball that satisfies `build_stack`) and the
**likelihood** dominated by the one real two-sided measurement — cosmic birefringence
(β = 0.34° ± 0.09°, mapped as β = 3.4·g_R2_parity), plus a one-sided sub-mm penalty.
(GW speed/dispersion are blind, v1.84, so contribute no two-sided pull.)

## Result — the data decisively favor the data-driven EFT

| framework | posterior | passes stack | β_pred |
|---|---|---|---|
| **discovered_data_driven** | **0.9992** | screened ✓ | **0.32°** |
| emergent_gravity (runner-up) | 0.0008 | — | 0° |
| string_tree_eft | ~0 | ✓ | 0° |
| asymptotic_safety | ~0 | ✓ | 0° |
| (all other catalogued) | ~0 | varies | 0° |

The data favor **`discovered_data_driven`** (the screened-scalaron + positive-handed
parity EFT) with **posterior 99.9%**, a **Bayes factor of ~1240** over the runner-up.
Every catalogued framework that predicts **β = 0** — string, asymptotic safety, CDT,
causal sets, … — is **effectively excluded** by the birefringence hint (β=0 is ~3.8σ from
0.34°). The stack-failers (LQG, Hořava, GFT) are additionally suppressed by the prior.

This is the full-posterior confirmation of v1.78 (the data *prefer* a nonzero, positive
parity coupling) and v1.79 (the data-driven EFT): no textbook framework matches the
birefringence signal; only a *purpose-built parity-violating* EFT does.

## Honest reading — partly by construction

The verdict is **partly circular**: `discovered_data_driven` was *constructed* (v1.79) to
match β = 0.34°, so its dominance is unsurprising. The robust, non-circular content is the
**counterfactual**: *if the cosmic-birefringence hint is real*, then the entire catalogue
of standard quantum-gravity frameworks (all predicting β = 0) is **decisively disfavored**,
and the data **demand** a new parity-violating theory beyond the textbook list. The Bayes
factor quantifies *how much* a real birefringence signal would cost the parity-even
frameworks: ~e⁷ per framework from the β term alone. The honest caveat carries through:
cosmic birefringence is a **~3.6σ hint**, not a discovery, and the β = 3.4·g_R2_parity map
is order-of-magnitude — so the *ranking* (parity-even excluded, parity-violating favored)
is robust, the precise 99.9% is not.
