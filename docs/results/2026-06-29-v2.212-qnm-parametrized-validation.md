# v2.212 - Operator->QNM sensitivity vs published parametrized-ringdown coefficients (an honest negative)

## Question

v2.210/v2.211 gave the engine a validated in-house WKB QNM solver and a numerically
*stable* operator->QNM sensitivity (scatter 2e-5). Before trusting that sensitivity for the
R4 ringdown route, it must be validated against a PUBLISHED higher-derivative QNM shift.
The cleanest target is the parametrized-ringdown framework of McManus, Berti, Macedo,
Kimura, Maselli, Cardoso (PRD 99 (2019) 104077, arXiv:1901.01265): it writes the modified
Regge-Wheeler potential in an explicit basis and tabulates the linear QNM-shift
coefficients. Does the in-house sensitivity reproduce them?

## Result

Artifact: `experiments/results/v2.212/qnm_parametrized_validation.json`.

Basis (McManus et al.): `delta_V = (1/r_H^2) sum_j alpha_j (r_H/r)^j`, r_H = 2M = 2 (M=1),
with `omega = omega_0 + sum_j alpha_j e_j`. So `d(omega)/d(alpha_j)` should equal `e_j`.
Tabulated (odd-parity gravitational, l=2, n=0), in M=1 units (= tabulated r_H*e_j / 2):

```text
        d(omega)/d(alpha_j) [WKB-at-peak]     e_j [McManus, exact]      rel.err
j=0     +0.31686 +0.07576 i                   +0.12362 +0.04632 i        1.48
j=1     +0.18792 +0.05005 i                   +0.07992 +0.00910 i        1.44
j=2     +0.10661 +0.04531 i                   +0.04832 -0.00121 i        1.54
```

**NEGATIVE: the WKB-at-peak sensitivity does NOT reproduce the published e_j.** It misses
them by ~150% -- a consistent ~2.5x on the real parts, with the imaginary parts diverging
(even the sign of e_2's imaginary part is wrong), so it is **not** a fixable normalization
or units factor. The v2.211 sensitivity is numerically *stable* but not *accurate*:
**stability != correctness.**

## Diagnosis

The exact first-order QNM sensitivity is a **global mode-overlap integral**,

```text
d(omega)/d(alpha_j) = integral psi_0^2 delta_V_j dr*  /  ( 2 omega_0 integral psi_0^2 dr* ),
```

over the (regularized) QNM eigenfunction `psi_0` -- this is what McManus et al. compute.
3rd-order WKB-at-peak only uses `delta_V_j`'s value and derivatives at the single
potential-peak point, so it captures the correct *structure* (finite, complex, j-dependent,
decreasing in magnitude with j) but is quantitatively wrong by O(1) factors for these
slowly-varying / horizon-weighted basis functions. The QNM *frequencies* themselves
(v2.210/v2.211, validated to ~0.2% across the Regge-Wheeler family) are unaffected; only the
peak-shortcut *sensitivity* is inaccurate.

This is the value of the test: it correctly bounds what the in-house tool can do. The
sensitivity machinery is a stable estimator of structure, **not a claim-grade operator->QNM
map**. The claim gate stays closed.

## Honest Scope

The QNM solver and its frequency predictions remain validated. What is shown inaccurate is
the *peak-derivative shortcut* for the sensitivity; a higher WKB order would not fix it (the
problem is peak-only-vs-global, not precision). The proper fix is the overlap-integral
sensitivity, which requires the QNM eigenfunction and the QNM-norm regularization
(Leung-Maassen-van den Brink) -- the content of v2.213.
