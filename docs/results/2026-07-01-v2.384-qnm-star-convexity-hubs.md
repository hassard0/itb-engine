# v2.384 - SWING (a tempting hypothesis fails honestly): star-centrality tracks a hub's hierarchy-safety, not its Chebyshev-centrality

## Question

v2.383 showed the consistent-EFT region is non-convex (pitted by swampland valleys near tiny couplings). The
natural rescue: is it at least **star-convex** around the constructed (Chebyshev-center) theory -- a "hub" from
which every consistent theory is reachable by a straight consistent path? Tempting hypothesis: the max-margin
point is the star-center.

## Result (the hypothesis fails, honestly)

```text
  constructed (Chebyshev center) star-fraction:        97%
  safe interior hubs (min coupling >= 0.03), n=34:      mean 94%   -- statistically the same as constructed
  unsafe hubs (a coupling near zero), n=6:              mean 74%   -- much worse
  corr(hub min-coupling, star-fraction):               0.90
```

All 5/5 checks pass. The constructed point **is** a good hub (97% of lines to random consistent theories stay
consistent) -- but it is **not specially good**: interior hierarchy-safe hubs average 94% (same within noise),
while hubs with a coupling at/near zero drop to 74%. Star-fraction correlates with the hub's smallest coupling
at 0.90.

## What this establishes

Star-centrality is governed by a hub's **hierarchy-safety** (how far its smallest coupling is from zero), not
by Chebyshev-centrality. The constructed theory is a good hub *only because* it happens to be hierarchy-safe
(smallest coupling 0.06, comfortably above the swampland floor); any interior hierarchy-safe theory is an
equally good hub, and the Chebyshev center earns no special star-status. And **no point is a perfect
star-center**: even the safest hubs lose ~4-6% of lines, because the swampland valleys (v2.383) pit the region
from every vantage -- to reach a near-zero-coupling consistent theory you must curve *around* the
hierarchy-forbidden region. This sharpens the v2.383 topology: the region is connected and "approximately
star-convex from the safe interior" but genuinely non-star-convex at the few-percent level, and the obstruction
is always the swampland hierarchy bound, never the amplitude sector. The honest upshot: the constructed
theory's specialness is its max-margin robustness (v2.322/361), **not** any central hub role -- the landscape
has no privileged center, only a safe interior and swampland-pitted edges.

## Honest scope

The star-fractions are Monte-Carlo estimates (finite lines per hub, sampled endpoint family), so the
percentages carry percent-level error and the safe/unsafe means are over a modest hub sample -- but the
ORDERING (safe hubs ~= constructed, unsafe hubs much worse) and the positive min-coupling/star-frac correlation
are robust and follow directly from the v2.383 mechanism (the SDC hierarchy bound is the only non-convex
constraint and bites near zero couplings). Inherits v2.383's scope: the non-star-convexity is real
(oracle-checked lines) but driven by the toy SDC aspect-ratio proxy and its near-zero-coupling treatment, so
the exact percentages are encoding-specific. "Hierarchy-safe" uses an arbitrary min-coupling threshold (~0.03);
the qualitative split is threshold-robust (unsafe hubs have a coupling ~0). This probes star-convexity
(necessary-not-sufficient for mapping the full shape). Robust content: star-centrality tracks hub
hierarchy-safety not Chebyshev-centrality; the constructed point is a good-but-not-special hub; no perfect
star-center exists. Toy SDC proxy, oracle-checked lines, MC percentages.
