# v1.35 — Fisher sloppiness: the new-physics freedom hides in the unmeasurable direction

**Date:** 2026-06-08
**Compute:** Vulcan.

## Context

v1.29 showed the exclusion-count priority metric saturates. The principled
replacement is the Fisher information metric of the observable set: which
*combinations* of Wilson coefficients can observations resolve (stiff), and which
are invisible (sloppy)? Because the engine's forward amplitudes are **linear** in
the coefficients, the Jacobian is constant and the Fisher matrix F = J^T J/σ² is
a single fixed matrix. Observables: matter (g_4 s²+g_6 s³+g_8 s⁴, σ=0.02),
graviton (g_R2 s²+g_R3 s³, σ=0.02), parity (g_R2_parity s²+g_R3_parity s³,
σ=0.005), over s∈[0.2,1.0].

## Result — a deeply sloppy model (condition number ≈ 1.4×10⁵)

Eigen-modes, stiff → sloppy:

| mode | relative eigenvalue | dominant content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (stiff) | 1.0 | parity (g_R2_parity, g_R3_parity) |
| 2 | 8.5×10⁻² | matter scale (g_4+g_6+g_8) |
| 3 | 6.2×10⁻² | graviton (g_R2, g_R3) |
| 4 | 6.7×10⁻³ | parity shape (g_R3_parity vs g_R2_parity) |
| 5 | 1.2×10⁻³ | matter shape (g_4 vs g_8) |
| 6–7 (invisible) | <5×10⁻⁴ | graviton shape; g_6 vs g_8 vs g_4 |

The eigenvalues span five orders of magnitude — a textbook sloppy model. The
matter-sector "shape" directions (separating g_4 from g_6 from g_8) are the
sloppiest: the monomials s², s³, s⁴ are nearly collinear over a finite energy
range, so a forward amplitude cannot disentangle the individual matter couplings.

## The cross-reference that matters: freedom vs resolution

| coefficient | freedom (constraint range, v1.33) | observational resolution | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **g_8** | 0.471 (loosest) | 0.453 | **degeneracy — loose but unmeasurable** |
| g_6 | 0.323 | 0.713 | degeneracy |
| g_4 | 0.293 | 0.274 | degeneracy |
| g_R2 | 0.237 | 0.076 | robust prediction (pinned & measurable) |
| g_R3 | 0.220 | 0.087 | robust prediction |
| g_R2_parity | 0.193 | 0.019 | robust prediction |
| g_R3_parity | 0.117 (tightest) | 0.022 | robust prediction |

This is the key, and it **corrects an earlier recommendation.** v1.27/v1.34
nominated **precision g_8** as the invented experiment that would reveal the
novel branches. The Fisher analysis says g_8 is exactly where observations are
*weakest*: the matter sector is both the loosest (constraints) and the sloppiest
(observations), so theories differing in g_8 are observationally near-degenerate
with known frameworks. **The new-physics freedom hides in the unmeasurable
direction.** Measuring "g_8" via a forward amplitude cannot isolate it from
g_4/g_6 — a structural collinearity, independent of precision.

Conversely, the **parity sector is both pinned (robust prediction) and the
stiffest, best-resolved mode.** So the honest re-ranking of the invented
experiments:

1. **Gravitational parity violation (g_R2_parity, g_R3_parity)** — the top
   testable new-physics signal: it is the most observationally accessible
   direction *and* the most tightly predicted (any detection above the pinned
   bound falsifies the whole consistent region). The discovered parity-violating
   branch (v1.31/v1.34, "the consistent cousin of LQG") is therefore the most
   experimentally actionable discovery of the program.
2. ~~Precision g_8~~ — **downgraded**: g_8 is loose but observationally
   degenerate; the novel weakly/strongly-coupled branches cannot be cleanly
   resolved by a single matter forward amplitude.

## Honest limitations

- The *stiffness ranking* depends on the assumed per-observable precisions (the
  parity observable was given σ=0.005 vs 0.02 for matter); a different precision
  budget reshuffles the top modes. What is **σ-independent** — and therefore the
  robust content — is the **matter-sector internal degeneracy**: g_4, g_6, g_8
  are mutually unresolvable from forward-amplitude shape no matter how precise the
  measurement, because s²/s³/s⁴ are collinear. Resolving them needs observables
  with different energy/kinematic dependence (multiple s-reaches, angular data).
- Linear-observable, toy-precision throughout.
