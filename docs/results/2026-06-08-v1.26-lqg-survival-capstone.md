# v1.26 — Capstone: comparative survival under joint uncertainty, and why LQG's exclusion is robust

**Date:** 2026-06-08
**Compute:** Vulcan (16-core, 4× V100). 400 000–500 000 draws/framework.
**Builds on:** v1.23–25 realism program.

---

## Context

The realism program watched LQG's proposed exclusion mechanism collapse three
times: RFC (v1.23, miscast artifact), BNOSSW (v1.23, form-fragile), forward
positivity (v1.25, inflated-g_R3 artifact). After v1.25, LQG's exclusion rested
on `generalized_anomaly_inflow` — and LQG sits at margin **+0.0012** on that
constraint at canonical values (a knife-edge). The obvious worry: **is
anomaly inflow the *next* artifact?** If LQG's last excluder is also fragile,
the honest conclusion flips to "the engine cannot exclude LQG at all."

This cycle settles it with two joint Monte-Carlo searches that admit **both**
sources of toy-ness at once — constraint prefactors *and* framework coefficients.

## Setup

- `anomaly_rho` made tunable (canonical 0.06, range [0.03, 0.12]).
- Stack: corrected convex-hull RFC + **geometric** (form-robust) BNOSSW +
  forward positivity + matter s³ — every known artifact/fragility removed.
- LQG coefficients sampled over Dr. M.'s ranges: g_4 pinned (±10%),
  g_6/g_8/g_R2 representative (±40%), g_R3 = ratio·g_R2 with ratio ∈ [0.1, 0.7]
  (physical spin-foam suppression), parity scaled ∈ [0.3, 1.5]× (Immirzi
  sensitivity). Constraint prefactors sampled over their plausible boxes.

## Result 1 — anomaly inflow is NOT a knife-edge; LQG is excluded redundantly

Joint LQG(coeff) × prefactor search, 500 000 draws:

> **LQG full-stack feasible in 1.50% of the joint space.**

The constraints excluding LQG, with the probability each is the *sole* violator
(i.e. removing it would flip the verdict):

| constraint | excludes LQG | sole excluder |
|---|---|---|
| scalar_wgc | 42.4% | 0.7% |
| dispersion_tower (g_6² ≤ g_4·g_8) | 39.8% | 0.3% |
| swampland_distance_conjecture | 39.1% | 2.2% |
| anomaly_cancellation | 38.2% | 0.6% |
| generalized_anomaly_inflow | 29.4% | 1.1% |
| complexity_cutoff | 29.2% | 0.4% |

**No constraint is the sole excluder more than ~2% of the time.** LQG is
excluded *redundantly* — many independent consistency conditions each disfavour
it, so deleting any one (including the knife-edge anomaly inflow) barely moves
the 98.5% exclusion. This is the opposite of the RFC/BNOSSW/forward-positivity
situation, where a single fragile constraint carried the whole verdict.

**The redundancy is the robustness.** No single "LQG fails because X" claim
survived the audit — yet "LQG fails" in aggregate is robust precisely because it
does not depend on any single X.

## Result 2 — comparative survival: LQG is an order of magnitude below the rest

Joint coeff × prefactor survival, 400 000 draws/framework, every framework
treated identically (Dr. M.'s pinned/representative model):

| framework | viable fraction of joint space | top excluders |
|---|---|---|
| **asymptotic safety** | **44.3%** | dispersion-tower 32%, matter-s³ 24%, scalar-WGC 23% |
| string tree-EFT | 23.5% | scalar-WGC 33%, forward-pos 33%, matter-s³ 25% |
| CDT | 19.1% | dispersion-tower 39%, anomaly-cancellation 32% |
| **LQG-induced** | **2.1%** | scalar-WGC 42%, dispersion-tower 40%, anomaly-cancellation 38% |

LQG is viable in ~2% of the joint plausible space — **roughly an order of
magnitude below every other framework**. The other three are comparably viable
(19–44%); their *ordering* is assumption-sensitive (string vs AS flips between
the canonical-prefactor view of v1.25 and this joint view, because string's
g_R3 is pinned-moderate while AS's is physically small), so the engine cannot
robustly rank string/AS/CDT at toy precision. But it **robustly disfavours
LQG**, by a wide margin, for the right reason: LQG's distinctive features
(parity violation, larger curvature couplings) trip many independent conditions
at once.

## The realism program's bottom line (v1.23–26)

After four cycles auditing both halves of the engine's toy-ness:

1. **A fully consistent theory exists** — robust (non-empty intersection in
   51–78% of prefactor space).
2. **LQG-induced is robustly disfavoured** — viable in ~2% of the joint
   uncertainty space, ~10× below string/AS/CDT — and robustly *because* the
   exclusion is redundant across many constraints, not because of any single
   (now-discredited) mechanism.
3. **The surviving frameworks cannot be ranked at toy precision** — string, AS,
   and CDT are comparably viable; which constraint binds, and which is "the"
   survivor, depends on modeling choices that the audit showed are not pinned.

Everything the engine can honestly claim today is (1)–(3). Reaching anything
sharper requires pinning specific coefficients — and the audit has named exactly
which ones matter (LQG parity/g_R3, AS g_6/g_8) and which constraints are
load-bearing vs artifact.

## Honest limitations

- The "~2%" depends on the chosen uncertainty ranges (factor-of-2 prefactors,
  Dr. M.'s coefficient ranges). It is a robustness *indicator*, not a
  probability of LQG being wrong. Different defensible ranges shift it, but the
  order-of-magnitude gap to the other frameworks is stable across the
  conditions tried.
- LQG's representative coefficients remain the dominant uncertainty. The single
  most valuable literature input is still the spin-foam/Immirzi determination of
  LQG's g_R3 and parity content.
