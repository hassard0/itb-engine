# v1.97 — The adversarial self-audit: which constraints are sole gatekeepers, and the theory that most nearly fools the stack

**Date:** 2026-06-08
**Compute:** Vulcan (16-core; 2M-sample bitmask cache).

---

## Probing the boundary of consistency

The Gödel test (v1.87) and constraint Jenga (v1.93) audited the engine's logic and
load-bearing structure. v1.97 audits its **boundary**: how close can a theory get to
consistency while still being excluded, and which constraints uniquely guard that
frontier?

## Result 1 — the exclusion shell is thick

Failure multiplicity (how many of 38 constraints each sample fails):

| fails | 0 (island) | 1 (near-miss) | 2 | … | 9 (peak) | … | 15 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| samples (of 2M) | 501 | 6 379 | 22 346 | … | **167 157** | … | 90 758 |

Most of theory space is **deeply** excluded — the distribution peaks at ~9
simultaneous failures. Only **0.3%** of samples are "near-miss" (fail exactly one
constraint). Consistency is not a thin membrane around a forbidden zone; it is a tiny
island surrounded by territory that violates *many* principles at once. This is *why*
the framework exclusions are so robust (v1.93): excluded theories miss by a mile, not
an inch.

## Result 2 — sole gatekeepers = irreplaceability (an identity)

For each constraint, the **sole-gatekeeper volume** = how many near-miss points it
*alone* excludes. There is an exact identity: removing constraint c admits precisely
the points whose only failure was c, so

> **V_without_c = V0 + gatekeeper(c)** ⟹ growth% = 100·gatekeeper(c)/V0.

The sole-gatekeeper ranking therefore *equals* the v1.93 irreplaceability (verified
numerically: distance conjecture 630%, anomaly inflow 312%, complexity 71%, … — matching
v1.93 within sampling). The top gatekeepers are the swampland (distance conjecture,
anomaly inflow, complexity, 't Hooft) plus the dispersion-tower, convexity, and
Hofman–Maldacena bounds.

**Constraints that *never* act alone** (gatekeeper = 0): the same ~22 — the entire
scalar-positivity tower, graviton/parity positivity, CEMZ, the holographic/info bounds,
LIGO, EFT-validity, causality, … Everything they exclude is *also* excluded by something
else, even at the very boundary. They are redundant not just globally but *at the
margin*.

## Result 3 — the theory that most nearly fools the stack

The **hardest near-miss** — the coefficient vector satisfying the most constraints with
the largest margins while still excluded:

```
g_4=0.567  g_6=0.392  g_8=0.400  g_R2=0.259  g_R3=0.143  g_C=0.375
g_R2_parity=0.058  g_R3_parity=-0.002
   sits 0.041 inside ALL of 37 constraints — killed ONLY by:
   >>> swampland_distance_conjecture <<<
```

A string-EFT-like theory with a moderate parity coupling, deep in the interior of every
bound *except* the swampland distance conjecture (its coefficient hierarchy is slightly
too large). The theory that comes closest to fooling the entire stack is undone by the
single most load-bearing principle — fitting, since the distance conjecture is the #1
sole gatekeeper.

## Honest scope

Near-miss volumes are sampling-resolution-dependent; the robust content is the **ranking**
of sole gatekeepers (= irreplaceability, an identity), the **thick exclusion shell**, and
the **hardest-near-miss point**.

## Headline

The consistent island is surrounded by a **thick exclusion shell** — most excluded
theories fail ~9 of 38 constraints at once, and only 0.3% are near-misses. The
**sole-gatekeeper volume is provably the v1.93 irreplaceability**, with the **swampland
distance conjecture** the dominant gatekeeper and ~22 constraints **never acting alone**.
And the theory that most nearly fools the whole stack — a string-EFT-like point deep
inside 37 of 38 bounds — is killed by exactly that one swampland condition.
