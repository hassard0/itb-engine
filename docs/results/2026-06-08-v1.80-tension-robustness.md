# v1.80 — Is the birefringence-vs-gravity tension robust? Stress-testing v1.79 against its toy mappings

**Date:** 2026-06-08
**Compute:** Vulcan (16-core; ~35M feasibility samples across the grid). Mechanism gut-checked with Dr. M.

---

## The question

v1.79's headline — anomaly inflow + unscreened sub-mm gravity cap cosmic
birefringence at β_max ≈ 0.09°, ~2.8σ below the measured 0.34° ± 0.09° — rests on
**two order-of-magnitude toy numbers**:

- **ρ_inflow** (anomaly-inflow strength, g_R2_parity² + 2g_R3_parity² ≤ ρ·g_4·g_R2),
  plausible range [0.03, 0.12];
- **κ_β** (parity coupling → birefringence angle, β = κ_β·g_R2_parity), plausible
  ~[2.0, 5.0] deg/unit.

The realism program demands: does the tension **survive** that uncertainty, or does
it need the canonical values? We sweep both.

## Method — a clean factorization

β_max = κ_β · g_R2_parity,max, and **g_R2_parity,max(ρ) depends on ρ but not κ_β**
(κ_β is only the conversion). So we compute g_R2_parity,max once per ρ (dense
parallel sampling of the consistent, sub-mm-safe region), then the whole (ρ, κ_β)
plane is a multiplication. Tension σ = (0.34 − β_max)/0.09.

## Result — robust across essentially the whole box

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| β_max range over the plausible box | **0.05° – 0.26°** |
| tension σ range | **0.94 – 3.2** |
| canonical (ρ=0.06, κ_β=3.4) | **2.5σ** |
| fraction of box in **>1σ** tension | **99.2%** |
| fraction of box in **>2σ** tension | **73%** |
| fraction reaching the measurement (<1σ) | **0.8%** |

- **Over 99% of the plausible (ρ, κ_β) box, unscreened modified gravity is in >1σ
  tension** with the cosmic birefringence; over 73% it is >2σ.
- **No reasonable corner reaches the measurement.** Only a sliver (~0.8%) at the
  *simultaneous* extreme — largest ρ (0.12) **and** largest κ_β (5.0) — drops below
  1σ. Everywhere else the consistent, sub-mm-safe EFT under-predicts β.
- The canonical tension lands at 2.5σ here (v1.79's single-point estimate was
  2.8σ; the difference is sampling — see caveats), bracketing a robust ~2.5–2.8σ.

**Verdict: the "birefringence prefers screening" conclusion is robust to the toy
mappings.** It does not hinge on the canonical ρ and κ_β; it fails only if *both*
are pushed to their most generous extremes at once.

## Why the mechanism is physical (Dr. M.)

The bound that drives this — the parity-odd (Chern–Simons / Pontryagin) coupling
bounded by the parity-even matter–curvature product — is not an arbitrary toy
choice. It is the **Green–Schwarz mechanism**: a Chern–Simons term cannot be added
freely; it must cancel a specific anomaly, so its magnitude is *dictated by the
anomaly coefficient of the existing spectrum*, not an independent parameter. And a
parity-odd sector parametrically larger than the parity-even one generically brings
ghosts, superluminality, or loss of unitarity — so "parity-violating bounded by
parity-conserving" is a healthy UV-completion expectation, not a stretch. The
logic then holds: a sub-mm-bounded (unscreened, small-g_R2) curvature sector
*forces* small birefringence, so a large measured β prefers either screening
(large g_R2 allowed) or a non-standard UV where the CS term is decoupled from
anomaly matching. (Pointer: Green–Schwarz / anomaly-mediated couplings in string
compactifications.)

## Honest caveats

- **g_R2_parity,max is a sampling LOWER bound** (random sampling finds feasible
  points but may miss the true maximum), so β_max is under-estimated and the
  tension σ is an **upper bound** — the true tension may be modestly *lower* than
  quoted. The qualitative conclusion (>1σ almost everywhere, no escape except the
  extreme corner) is stable to this; the exact 73%/0.8% carry sampling uncertainty
  (visible as per-ρ striping in the heatmap).
- This is still a **3.6σ birefringence HINT**, and the screened/unscreened
  dichotomy simplifies density-dependent screening (v1.77). The robust content is
  structural: anomaly inflow ties β to a sub-mm-bounded sector across the whole
  plausible mapping range.
