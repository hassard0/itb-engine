# v2.312 - What framework does the engine prefer? Constructing the most-robustly-consistent EFT

## Question

Taking the new-theory mandate literally: instead of testing community frameworks, ask what framework the
engine's consistency conditions actually PREFER -- the most robustly feasible point, maximizing the
worst-case constraint margin (the Chebyshev-center idea) over the whole 7-coupling space -- and propose it
as a candidate.

## Result (the engine constructs a nonzero candidate, distinct from every input)

**pure GR is feasible but marginal.** At the origin, 29 of the 38 constraints saturate simultaneously
(homogeneous positivity / causality / swampland forms that vanish at zero couplings), so pure GR sits on
29 walls with worst-case margin exactly 0.

**But the feasible set has a real interior, and its center is a new framework.** A multi-seed
coordinate-ascent search finds a strictly-interior point with all 38 margins positive (worst-case margin
+0.0063):

```text
  engine-preferred framework:
    g_4 = 0.57   g_6 = 0.40   g_8 = 0.35   g_R2 = 0.19   g_R3 = 0.10   parity = 0
```

This Chebyshev center is a string-tree-EFT-like matter sector (g_4, g_6, g_8 near the string values
0.5/0.4/0.4) with TRIMMED curvature couplings (g_R2, g_R3 pulled below string's 0.2/0.15 into the feasible
interior). It is more robust than pure GR (strictly interior vs marginal on 29 walls) and feasible where
all four community frameworks are infeasible:

```text
  framework             worst-case margin   feasible
  engine-preferred      +0.0063             yes (strictly interior)
  pure_gr                0.0000             yes (marginal, on 29 walls)
  string_tree_eft       -0.0400             no
  asymptotic_safety     -0.0525             no
  cdt                   -0.0484             no
  lqg_induced           -0.1200             no
```

All 7/7 checks pass.

## What this establishes

The engine does not merely tolerate GR and reject the candidates -- it points to a concrete NEW framework
at the most-interior point of the carved region: keep the string-like matter sector, but soften the
curvature corrections until every consistency wall clears. That is the engine's own answer to "what
higher-derivative gravity is most robustly consistent," and it matches no framework on the menu. It also
corrects the picture of the feasible region: it is not a measure-zero skin around pure GR but a genuine
(small) interior whose center sits at nonzero, string-like-but-less-curved couplings.

## Honest scope

The decisive claims are SIGN-based and independent of the heterogeneous margin scales: "pure GR feasible
but on 29 walls," "a point with all 38 margins strictly positive exists" (nonempty interior), "the
preferred point is strictly feasible and nonzero," and "no community higher-derivative framework is
feasible" depend only on margin signs, not normalization. The "worst-case margin" magnitude mixes units,
so the EXACT preferred point is the argmax of a scale-dependent objective and is not canonical -- a
different robustness metric would move it; what is robust is that some strictly-interior point exists, it
is nonzero, more robust than pure GR, and unlike any community framework. The interior point was found by
multi-seed coordinate ascent: an earlier coarse random scan missed it and wrongly suggested an EMPTY
interior -- corrected here (the optimum is approximate, grid-quantized, and seed-dependent in its exact
coordinates). Default 38-constraint stack, no opt-in extensions; couplings restricted to g >= 0. The
"string-like matter, trimmed curvature" reading is an interpretation of one approximate optimum, not a
derived UV completion. Toy basis, O(1) prefactors.
