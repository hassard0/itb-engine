# v2.350 - A cross-sector inequality: cosmic birefringence puts a LOWER bound on the parity-EVEN matter x curvature product

## Question

A genuine swing rather than another audit (the mandated "cross-sector moment" direction). The program has
two exact parity-sector facts pointing in opposite directions -- can they be chained into a new corollary in
a *different* sector?

## Result (yes -- a parity observable floors the parity-even sector)

Two exact constraints:
- **birefringence (DATA)** bounds the parity coupling from below: `g_R2_parity >= (beta - n*sigma)/kappa =
  0.0471` (2-sigma).
- **anomaly inflow** bounds it from above through the parity-EVEN couplings: `g_R2_parity^2 <= rho*g_4*g_R2`.

Chaining eliminates the parity coupling entirely:

```text
  g_4 * g_R2  >=  g_R2_parity^2 / rho  >=  (beta_lower)^2 / rho  ~  0.0369

  derived bound  g_4*g_R2 >= 0.03691
  constructed    g_4*g_R2  = 0.1021   (margin +0.065,  ~2.8x)
  family min     g_4*g_R2  = 0.0427   (margin +0.006,  nearly tight at the family edge)
  bound WITHOUT birefringence data:  0.0
```

So a *parity* observable (cosmic birefringence), routed through the anomaly inflow, places a *lower* bound on
the *parity-EVEN* leading-matter x leading-curvature product. The engine confirms it: across the whole
consistent+observed family the minimum `g_4*g_R2` stays above the derived bound -- and is **nearly tight** at
the family edge (0.0427 vs 0.0369), so this is an *active* cross-sector constraint, not a loose one. All 5/5
checks pass.

## What this establishes

A theory that **matches** the cosmic-birefringence detection cannot have an arbitrarily weak leading matter
coupling or leading curvature coupling -- their product is floored. The result is genuinely **data-sourced**:
without the detection the parity lower edge drops to ~0 (the measurement excludes zero at ~3.8 sigma, so its
own ~3.8-sigma edge reaches `g_R2_parity ~ 0`) and the bound vanishes -- it is the *detection*, not the
operator content, that floors the parity-even product. This is a new consequence in a **different sector**
than the parity coupling the data obviously constrains, and it is the cross-sector companion to the parity
headline: the same anomaly inflow that v2.335 used to couple the two parity-odd couplings also couples the
parity-odd DATA to the parity-even matter+curvature sector.

## Honest scope

The corollary is **exact algebra** given the two engine constraints (a Cauchy-Schwarz-style chain, no
approximation), and the feasible family respecting it is a genuine numerical confirmation -- but both input
constraints are toy-basis encodings: the birefringence map (`beta = 3.4 deg * g_R2_parity`, data 0.34 +/-
0.09 deg) and the anomaly-inflow form + its prefactor `rho` (the v2.344 load-bearing prefactor, default
0.06). So the **number** 0.0369 scales as `1/rho` and as `beta_lower^2` -- an O(1) change in either moves it
-- and the bound is only as firm as the cosmic-birefringence detection (the ~3.6-sigma hint, v2.329 caveat:
if it is a systematic, `beta_lower -> 0` and the bound disappears). The **robust** content is the structure:
a parity observable, via anomaly inflow, lower-bounds the parity-even matter x curvature product, with a
magnitude set by `(data lower edge)^2 / (anomaly prefactor)`. The family check is a seeded random-walk sample
(sampled, not proven), but the inequality is analytic so no feasible point can violate it regardless. Toy
basis, O(1) prefactors.
