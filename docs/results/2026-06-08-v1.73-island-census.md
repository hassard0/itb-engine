# v1.73 — How constrained is a consistent quantum-gravity EFT? The dimensionality of the island

**Date:** 2026-06-08
**Compute:** Vulcan (16-core, multiprocessing; 300k samples in 6.4 s wall).

---

## The question

Every prior cycle excluded *parts* of theory-space. v1.73 asks the global,
core-mission question directly: **how rare, and how low-dimensional, is the set of
consistent quantum-gravity EFTs?** We Monte-Carlo the coefficient space against the
full corrected stack and measure the surviving "island": its volume, its
dimensionality (PCA), the walls that define its boundary, and where real
frameworks sit within it.

## Honest setup (the box matters)

A full uniform 8-D box yields **~0 survivors** — but for an *artifactual* reason:
the Distance Conjecture forbids coefficient hierarchies > 20×, and a uniformly
near-zero parity coefficient against an O(0.5) matter coefficient trivially
violates it. So we census the **parity-even slice** (g_R2_parity = g_R3_parity =
0), where 11 of 12 frameworks live, with **comparable-magnitude** ranges so the
Distance and Complexity walls are genuinely satisfiable:

| coeff | range | | coeff | range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| g_4 | [0.05, 0.6] | | g_R3 | [0.0, 0.4] |
| g_6 | [0.05, 0.6] | | g_C | [0.02, 0.6] |
| g_8 | [0.05, 0.7] | | g_R2_parity, g_R3_parity | 0 |
| g_R2 | [0.02, 0.45] | | | |

The volume fraction is meaningful **only relative to this box** — it is not an
absolute "probability of consistency."

## Result 1 — the island is thin

**Volume fraction = 0.60% ± 0.01%** (1789 survivors / 300 000; binomial s.e.).
A consistent higher-derivative gravitational EFT is a ~1-in-167 point of an
otherwise reasonable, comparable-magnitude coefficient box. Dr. M.'s read: this is
the swampland statement made quantitative — the landscape is a thin subset of EFT
space.

## Result 2 — the island is mildly sloppy, ~3.4 effective dimensions

PCA of the standardized survivors (6 varying coordinates):

| PC | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| eigenvalue | 2.83 | 1.11 | 0.77 | 0.69 | 0.36 | 0.25 |
| cumulative var | 47% | 66% | 78% | 90% | 96% | 100% |

- **Participation ratio = 3.4 effective dimensions** (of 6). The island is *not*
  collapsed to a line, nor is it a full-dimensional blob — it is mildly **sloppy**.
- **One dominant "stiff" direction** (PC1, 47% of variance) is a **collective-scale
  mode**: it loads g_4 (−0.51), g_6 (−0.48), g_R2 (−0.51), g_R3 (−0.38), g_C
  (−0.33) **together** — the matter and graviton couplings must grow and shrink in
  concert. Deviating along this overall-magnitude axis is what most quickly leaves
  the island. Dr. M.: the stiff collective mode is the classic fingerprint of
  intersecting half-space (positivity/causality) constraints — the same
  "sloppy-model" structure (Sethna et al.) the engine saw locally in its v1.35
  Fisher analysis, now seen globally.
- **g_8 is decoupled** from the stiff mode (PC1 loading −0.01). Physically expected:
  positivity bites hardest on the *lowest* Mandelstam moments (g_4, g_6); the s⁴
  moment g_8 is the most weakly tied to the curvature sector.

## Result 3 — the walls of the island

Two different "wall" censuses, both informative:

- **What most often excludes a random EFT** (primary excluder of the 298k
  failures): **`hofman_maldacena_wedge` (66 737)** — the v1.71 a/c wedge is the
  single most common reason a random EFT is inconsistent — then `cft_flat_space`
  (53 630), `complexity_cutoff` (40 622), `graviton_forward_positivity` (31 272),
  `scalar_wgc` (27 078), `distance_conjecture` (23 755). The newest constraint is
  doing the most work.
- **What bounds the survivors** (nearest-margin wall *inside* the island):
  **`generalized_anomaly_inflow` (1418 / 1789 = 79%)** — most survivors sit closest
  to the anomaly-inflow boundary, then `bekenstein_tight` (125). So the island's
  *surface* is mostly the anomaly-inflow wall, even though that wall rarely makes
  the *primary* exclusion — survivors have already passed everything else and live
  pressed against anomaly inflow.

## Result 4 — real frameworks cluster near the centroid

Distance (standardized) of each framework to the island centroid:

| near centre (feasible) | z-dist | | far / outliers | z-dist | feasible |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| causal_set | 1.16 | | discovered_parity_violating | 5.94 | ✗ |
| asymptotic_safety | 1.42 | | group_field_theory | 6.18 | ✗ |
| discovered_high_g8 | 2.20 | | lqg_induced | 6.91 | ✗ |
| string_tree_eft | 2.51 | | pure_gr | 8.17 | ✓ (trivial, scale-outlier) |
| cdt | 2.78 | | horava_lifshitz | 11.0 | ✗ |

The **surviving** real QG candidates sit in the dense core (z ≲ 2.8); the
**excluded** frameworks are far outliers (z ≳ 5.9). Pure GR is feasible but a
scale-outlier (all-small trivial point).

**Honest skeptical read (Dr. M.):** "consistent theories cluster near the centroid"
is a *semi-tautology* — string theory satisfies the axioms, so of course it is in
the island. The non-trivial content is in the **exclusion**: LQG, GFT, and
Hořava are pushed out not by "they're not string theory" but by general bounds
(causality, anomaly inflow, the a/c wedge) they themselves claim to respect. The
clustering is a sanity check; the outliers are the physics.

## Headline

In a comparable-magnitude box, the consistent quantum-gravity EFT island is
**0.60% by volume**, **~3.4-dimensional**, organized around **one stiff
collective-scale direction** (with g_8 decoupled), bounded at its surface by
**anomaly inflow**, and most often entered only after passing the **a/c
conformal-collider wedge** — the program's newest constraint, which turns out to
be the most frequent excluder of a random EFT. The consistent frameworks live in
its core; the excluded ones are outliers, by general principle.
