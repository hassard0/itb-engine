g_R² up, because nothing was pulling it down. Adding the Distance

aspect ratios.

### Pattern 2: Single-framework discrimination is robust to constraint accumulation

Across all 6 iterations, the persistent framework failure remains:

This is the single failure mode that has held since v1.1. None of my
new additions (QFC, spin-4, GSL, distance conjecture) eliminated any
new framework, or saved LQG-induced. The class-B constraint that
distinguishes holographic from non-holographic theories continues to
be the binding constraint for the parity-violating framework.

### Pattern 3: The architecture handles steady accretion cleanly

12 → 28 active constraints, 4 → 5 frameworks, 295 → 330 tests, all in
six iterations with zero core refactors. This is the strongest signal
yet that the engine architecture is research-grade: it accommodates
constraint additions at the rate a real research group would add them.

## What this means for the actual physics question

After 28 constraints across 7 Wilson coefficients with 5 candidate
frameworks tested:

1. **Three of the five frameworks are observationally close** in
   coefficient space: string tree EFT, asymptotic safety, CDT. Their
   pairwise fingerprint distances are 0.2–0.4. They are distinguishable
   in principle by high-precision experiments but probably not by
   current ones.

2. **Pure GR is at the trivial origin** — it satisfies every constraint
   with margin ≥ 0 by construction (every multiplicative constraint is
   trivially zero).

3. **LQG-induced is the lone outlier** — it fails three independent
   constraints (BNOSSW MMI, strict-anomaly, complexity cutoff) at toy
   values, and its non-zero parity content puts it close to the
   distance-conjecture aspect-ratio bound.

4. **The engine optimum sits near the cluster centroid** of string/AS/CDT.
   This is the engine's predicted "best-fit UV completion" given current
   encodings — a parity-conserving theory with matter coefficients near
   string-tree-level magnitudes but slightly tilted toward CDT's pattern.

## Dr. M.'s recommendation

**Stop adding new constraints. Start replacing toy prefactors with
publication-grade numerical values.**

The diminishing returns are clear: my last five proposals all passed
without producing any framework-discrimination. The engine has the
right *structural* coverage; what it lacks is *numerical* precision.
Specifically:

1. Replace `α_QFC = 0.5` with the literature-derived prefactor (Bousso
   2015 §4 gives this to leading order).
2. Replace `κ` in mixed-positivity bounds with Caron-Huot et al 2024
   sharp boundary results.
3. Replace `c_max = 1.5` in complexity cutoff with a Lloyd-bound-derived
   numerical scale (which depends on Λ).
4. Replace `bound = 0.1` in LIGO bounds with the actual published
   sensitivity numbers in proper units.
5. Replace toy framework values (string g_4 = 0.5) with literature
   values for the corresponding string tree-level α' coefficients.

Each of these is **literature-aware encoding work**, not architectural
invention. The engine is in working order. The next move belongs to
careful literature reading and faithful numerical encoding.
