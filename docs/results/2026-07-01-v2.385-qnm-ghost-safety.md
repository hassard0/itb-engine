# v2.385 - SWING (the #1 objection): the constructed theory's Ostrogradsky ghost is kept above the EFT cutoff by the WGC

## Question

The most-cited objection to higher-derivative gravity is the Ostrogradsky instability: the Weyl^2 term
propagates a massive spin-2 **ghost**. Does the constructed theory suffer it -- and if so, is the ghost above
the EFT cutoff (safe) or below (fatal)?

## Result (ghost above cutoff, guaranteed by the WGC)

The Weyl^2 coupling `g_C` sources a ghost at `m_g^2 ~ Lambda^2 / g_C`, so the ghost is above the cutoff (a
truncation artifact, not a low-energy instability) exactly when `g_C < 1`, i.e. `m_g/Lambda = 1/sqrt(g_C) > 1`.

```text
  constructed:  g_C = g_R2 = 0.193  ->  m_g/Lambda = 2.28   (ghost at ~2.3x the cutoff)
  whole region: g_C <= 0.254 < 1    ->  m_g/Lambda >= 1.99  (never within 2x of the cutoff)
  WGC g_R2 <= sqrt(g_4) holds (True); g_4 < 1 (max 0.63)  ->  forces g_C < 1
```

All 5/5 checks pass. The entire feasible region keeps the ghost parametrically above the cutoff.

## What this establishes

Higher-derivative gravity's headline pathology is **not fatal** here: the constructed theory is a healthy EFT
(ghost above cutoff), and the guarantee is the **Weak Gravity Conjecture**. The engine's WGC is
`g_R2 <= sqrt(g_4)`, and with `g_4 < 1` across the feasible region this forces `g_C < 1` -- the ghost above the
cutoff. So the **same weak-gravity condition that makes extremal black holes decay (v2.378) also protects the
theory from its own higher-derivative ghost** -- the Ostrogradsky instability is kept trans-cutoff by
quantum-gravity consistency, not by hand. The deeper resolution is the **infinite tower (v2.375)**: a
finite-derivative truncation carries the ghost as a genuine pole, but the full string-like log-convex tower is
what a ghost-free UV completion (string theory, non-local gravity) looks like from the EFT side, so the
trans-cutoff ghost is precisely the truncation artifact the tower resolves. This is the consistent-EFT answer
to "but higher-derivative gravity has ghosts."

## Honest scope

The relation `m_g^2 ~ Lambda^2/g_C` carries an O(1) convention factor (a 2 or 6 depending on the normalization
of `C^2` and the cutoff), so `m_g/Lambda = 1/sqrt(g_C)` is O(1)-schematic -- the ROBUST, parametric content is
`g_C < 1 => ghost above the cutoff`, satisfied with room to spare (`g_C <= 0.25`, `m_g/Lambda >= 2`). `g_C`
(Weyl^2) is identified with `g_R2` in the engine's toy basis; the physical ghost is specifically the Weyl^2
(spin-2) one, so a genuine basis separating Weyl^2 from Ricci^2 could shift which coupling carries it -- here
`g_R2` stands in for `g_C`. "Ghost above cutoff = safe" is the standard EFT stance: it shows the ghost is not a
low-energy pathology, NOT that a specific unitary UV completion exists (though the tower, v2.375, is the
natural candidate). The WGC used is the engine's simplified `g_R2 <= sqrt(g_4)` (v2.378 scope); `g_4 < 1` is an
empirical feature of the feasible region (max 0.63), robust but not a single hard constraint. Robust content:
the feasible region keeps the Weyl^2 ghost parametrically above the cutoff, guaranteed by the WGC -- the
headline ghost is trans-cutoff here, not fatal. O(1)-schematic mass, toy `g_C=g_R2`, EFT-sense safety.
