## The idea

v1.44 placed the gravitational cutoff at the dark-energy scale (2.4 meV). The
natural occupant of that scale is a **quintessence axion** φ — mass ~ Hubble,
energy density ~ ρ_Λ. A single such field generically does three things at once:

1. **drives cosmic acceleration** (it is the dark-energy field);
2. **rotates CMB polarization** via L ⊃ (c_γ α / 4π f) φ F F̃ → **cosmic
   birefringence**, the observed ~3σ Minami–Komatsu hint (β ≈ 0.34° ± 0.09°);
3. **sources gravitational parity** via the θ-term (c_grav/f) φ R R̃ →
   Chern–Simons gravity = **the engine's g_R2_parity**, its parity-violating
   frontier (the "consistent cousin of LQG").

If one field does all three, the engine's most exotic prediction connects to a
real observational anomaly, anchored by the cosmological constant. Do the
magnitudes hang together?

## They do

**Cosmic birefringence is the natural O(α) signal of a dark-energy axion.** The
rotation is β = ½ (c_γ α/π)(Δφ/f). The observed β = 0.34° requires
c_γ·(Δφ/f) ≈ 5.1 (range 3.8–6.5) — an O(few) anomaly coefficient with an O(1)
field excursion:

| c_γ (Δφ/f = 1) | β |
|---|---|
| 1 | 0.07° |
| 2 | 0.13° |
| **5** | **0.33°  ← matches observed** |
| 10 | 0.67° |

So β is *generically* O(0.1°): the observed 0.34° is the expected magnitude of a
dark-energy axion, not a tuned coincidence.

**The same axion's gravitational coupling lands in the engine's allowed range.**
The engine's parity-violating branch has |g_R2_parity| = 0.092 — an O(0.1)
gravitational θ-coupling. Both the electromagnetic (c_γ ~ few) and gravitational
(O(0.1)) anomaly coefficients are O(0.1–few): mutually plausible for a common
axion with comparable anomaly coefficients (the precise ratio is model-dependent
and only order-of-magnitude here — the EM coupling carries an explicit α, the
gravitational one does not, so they are not directly equal).

## The correlated, falsifiable prediction

If one dark-energy axion sources both, then **alongside the observed CMB
(electromagnetic) birefringence there must be a gravitational (GW) birefringence**
set by |g_R2_parity| ~ 0.09. From the v1.41 spec sheet, flagging it needs GW
birefringence sensitivity ~0.01 (≈10× below LIGO O3) — LIGO O5 / next-generation
detectors.

> **The joint signature:** cosmic birefringence (electromagnetic, *already seen*
> at ~3σ) + GW birefringence (gravitational, *predicted*, LIGO-O5-testable) +
> sub-mm gravity (v1.41) + dark energy — all anchored at the 2.4 meV scale.
> **Detecting the gravitational partner of the CMB rotation would tie cosmic
> acceleration to quantum-gravitational parity violation in one stroke.**

This is the most far-reaching falsifiable statement the program has produced: it
predicts that the engine's parity-violating frontier and a real cosmological
anomaly are two faces of the same dark-energy field, and it says where to look
for the missing face (GW birefringence at LIGO O5).

## Honest limitations

- The link between the electromagnetic (cosmic birefringence) and gravitational
  (engine parity) couplings **assumes a common axionic origin** — generic in
  string/axiverse UV completions, but not guaranteed; they are independent
  anomaly coefficients in general.
- Cosmic birefringence is a **~3σ hint**, not a confirmed detection; systematics
  (e.g. polarization-angle calibration) are still under scrutiny.
- The numerology is order-of-magnitude; β ∝ c_γ·(Δφ/f) is degenerate between the
  anomaly coefficient and the field excursion.

What is solid: (i) the observed β is the *natural scale* of a dark-energy axion;
(ii) the engine's parity branch is an O(0.1) gravitational θ-coupling; (iii) a
common origin makes a sharp, near-term, falsifiable prediction (correlated GW
birefringence). The hypothesis is now a target, not a story.
