# v2.304 - Is the consistent theory space convex? A non-convexity counterexample

## Question

A deliberate pivot from the "which principle bounds which coupling" sub-arc to a STRUCTURAL property of
the carved region: is it CONVEX? Convexity is physically meaningful -- it means a mixture (average) of two
consistent theories is itself consistent. Does the engine's feasible region have it?

## Result (NON-convex: a verified counterexample)

It does not. Two theories, both satisfying the engine's repulsive-force conjecture
`g_4 g_6 - g_R2 - g_R2^2 >= 0`, have an infeasible midpoint:

```text
  theory                          g_4    g_6    g_R2    repulsive-force margin
  A                               1.00   0.30   0.220   +0.0316   feasible
  B                               0.20   0.06   0.010   +0.0019   feasible
  midpoint (A+B)/2                0.60   0.18   0.115   -0.0202   INFEASIBLE
```

A 50/50 mixture of two consistent theories is INCONSISTENT -- the feasible region bulges inward along
this direction. The cause is the BILINEAR matter product `g_4 g_6`: along a segment between two asymmetric
points the product dips below the chord (`(0.6)(0.18)=0.108 < 0.5(0.3)+0.5(0.012)=0.156`), so the
midpoint's matter sector can no longer support its (averaged) curvature.

This is in sharp contrast to the amplitude-positivity sector. The curvature moment tower
(`g_R3^2 <= g_R2 g_R4`) and the matter dispersion tower (`g_6^2 <= g_4 g_8`) are rotated second-order
cones; averaging two feasible points stays feasible (verified) -- those constraints are CONVEX. All 5
consistency checks pass.

## What this establishes

Convexity is the exact dividing line between the engine's two kinds of constraint:

```text
  CONVEX     forward-dispersion / moment / positivity bounds   (Hankel-PSD, second-order cones)
  NON-CONVEX gravitational swampland-style bounds              (bilinear products g_4 g_6: WGC / repulsive force)
```

Physically: amplitude positivity carves a convex cone of allowed coefficients (the "EFT-hedron" is a
convex positive geometry), but the gravitational consistency conditions carve a NON-convex region. The
swampland is not the convex complement of a convex landscape, and the intuition that "interpolating
between two consistent quantum gravities stays consistent" FAILS once gravity (WGC / repulsive force) is
imposed. That is a genuine structural property of the carved region -- invisible from any single bound,
and only exposed by testing mixtures.

## Honest scope

The non-convexity is EXACT for the engine's encoded repulsive-force conjecture (the bilinear `g_4 g_6`
product is the literal form): the A, B, midpoint margins are the engine's verdict and the counterexample
is reproducible (a dedicated test rebuilds it independently). A and B are tested against the
repulsive-force constraint specifically -- not claimed feasible against all 38 constraints -- but that
suffices: one genuine engine constraint being non-convex makes the full feasible region (an intersection)
non-convex along that direction. The convexity of the moment / dispersion towers is exact (rotated
second-order cones are convex by construction). The structural conclusion (positivity convex,
gravitational bilinear bounds non-convex) is prefactor-robust: it follows from the FORM of the
constraints, not the O(1) constants. The "swampland is non-convex" reading is the standard interpretation
of WGC-type product bounds; here made concrete and verified on the engine. Toy basis, O(1) prefactors.
