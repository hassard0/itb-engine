## What this establishes

Post-v2.462, the gravitational observables are **internally consistent** with their cited physics. The one stale
item is the sub-mm / Yukawa observable: it models a meV-scale R² scalaron fifth force — the old
scalaron-as-dark-energy picture that v2.457–459 retired (the R² scalaron is the *heavy* inflaton, giving no sub-mm
force; the dark energy is the ultralight axion). But the sub-mm constraint is **non-binding** on the candidate
(margin = 1.0, fully satisfied), so this stale assumption does **not** affect the feasible region or the candidate
— a harmless remnant, **flagged for eventual cleanup, not removed** (removing an inert constraint would risk a
feasible-region change for zero benefit).

Net: the cross-module cross-check is now a standing practice that caught a real factor-of-2 error which had
persisted — and the sweep confirms no other binding observable bug.

## Honest scope

A **code-consistency audit**, not a new physics result. "OK" means the observable's formula matches its cited
physics and is internally consistent — it does *not* upgrade any of them from their toy/what-if tier (the
holographic ones remain contingent on an assumed AdS dual with an order-of-magnitude lam_map). The audit is *not
exhaustive* — it covered the `gravitational_observables` module and `holographic_ac` functions by cross-checking
formulas against cited results and each other; it did not re-derive every constraint. "No new binding bugs" means
no further formula error affecting a *binding* constraint. The sub-mm "inert" call rests on the margin = 1.0
non-binding check (specific to the current candidate + stack); if a future change made sub-mm binding, the stale
scalaron assumption would need fixing first.
