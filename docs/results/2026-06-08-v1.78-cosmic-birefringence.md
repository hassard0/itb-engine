# v1.78 — The second experiment: cosmic birefringence, and the engine's first data preference for a nonzero (parity-violating) coefficient

**Date:** 2026-06-08
**Compute:** Vulcan. Measurement values read/cited from the published papers (Minami–Komatsu; Eskilt–Komatsu).

## A different kind of data

v1.77 ingested an *exclusion* (Eöt-Wash sub-mm gravity) in the matter sector. v1.78
ingests a *positive measurement* in the **parity** sector: the isotropic cosmic
birefringence angle

        β = 0.34° ± 0.09°

(Minami & Komatsu, PRL 125, 221301 (2020): 0.35° ± 0.14°, Planck PR3;
Eskilt & Komatsu 2022: 0.34° ± 0.09°, Planck PR4 + WMAP) — a **~3.6σ hint** of a
parity-violating Chern–Simons / Pontryagin coupling. Unlike every prior constraint,
this one **prefers a nonzero coefficient**.

`CosmicBirefringenceData` (`src/itb/constraints/cosmic_birefringence.py`) maps the
leading toy parity coupling to the angle, β = κ_β·g_R2_parity with κ_β = 3.4 °/unit
(so an O(0.1) coupling gives the measured O(0.3°); normalization is
order-of-magnitude), and enforces the two-sided band |β_pred − 0.34°| ≤ n_σ·0.09°.
It's wired into `build_stack` behind `include_birefringence=False` (default off,
theoretical-only stack unchanged — suite **452 green**), with a `mode` flag
(hint / confirmed / ignore).

## The engine now prefers a parity-violating universe

- **β = 0 (parity-even) is excluded at 3.78σ.** The preferred g_R2_parity band
  (2σ) is **[0.047, 0.153]** — it does **not** contain zero. For the first time, an
  ingested measurement makes the engine *prefer a nonzero coupling*.
- And it selects a **handedness**: β > 0 requires g_R2_parity > 0. A parity-odd
  universe of the *wrong* sign is just as disfavored as a parity-even one.

## What it does to the frameworks — three lessons

| framework | g_R2_parity | β_pred (°) | birefringence | theory-consistent? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **hořava_lifshitz** | 0.10 | **0.34** (bullseye) | ✓ in band | ✗ |
| **lqg_induced** | 0.08 | 0.27 | ✓ in band | ✗ |
| **group_field_theory** | 0.07 | 0.24 | ✓ in band | ✗ |
| discovered_high_g8 | 0.033 | 0.11 | ✗ (too small) | ✓ |
| discovered_novel | 0.021 | 0.07 | ✗ (too small) | ✓ |
| string / cdt / asymp-safety / causal-set / pure-GR / emergent | 0.0 | 0.0 | ✗ (parity-even) | ✓ |
| **discovered_parity_violating** | **−0.092** | **−0.31** | ✗ (**wrong sign**) | ✓ |

1. **The "respectable" frameworks predict zero birefringence** and are now
   disfavored at 3.78σ — string-EFT, CDT, asymptotic safety, causal sets, pure GR
   all sit at β = 0.
2. **The engine's own discovered_parity_violating framework has the WRONG
   handedness** (β = −0.31°): it *is* parity-violating, but of the opposite sign to
   the data, so it is disfavored. Data does not just prefer "parity violation" — it
   picks a side, and this framework is on the wrong one.
3. **The birefringence-favored frameworks (Hořava, LQG, GFT) all fail the
   theoretical stack** (Lorentz violation / EFThedron / GB causality). They predict
   the right angle for the wrong reasons.

## The 2-experiment empirical swampland: no framework survives

Combining theory + sub-mm (v1.77) + cosmic birefringence (v1.78):

> **survive theory + both data = ∅** (zero of the 12 toy frameworks).

No hand-built framework simultaneously (i) passes the theoretical consistency
stack, (ii) has a light-enough scalaron to evade sub-mm gravity (g_R2 < 0.063), and
(iii) has a positive-handed parity coupling g_R2_parity ≈ 0.1 to match cosmic
birefringence. The frameworks that match the birefringence are theory-excluded; the
theory-consistent ones predict zero or wrong-sign birefringence and (mostly)
too-heavy scalarons.

**This is the most pointed result of the data arc.** The two experiments together
point at a *specific kind of EFT* — theory-consistent, sub-mm-safe (heavy scalaron),
**and** positive-parity-violating — that **none of the textbook quantum-gravity
frameworks occupy**. The empirical swampland is telling us the toy frameworks are
all missing something, and naming what: a consistent theory with a definite-handed
Chern–Simons coupling and a heavy curvature scalaron.

## Honesty

- Cosmic birefringence is a **~3.6σ hint, not a discovery**. The dominant systematic
  is the **miscalibration of detector polarization angles**; the EB-nulling and
  foreground-EB techniques mitigate but do not eliminate it. If it evaporates, this
  constraint should be set to `mode="ignore"`. We provide that flag rather than
  hard-coding the preference.
- The β ↔ g_R2_parity normalization (κ_β = 3.4) is order-of-magnitude; the robust
  content is **sign + nonzero**, not the precise coupling value.
- Like sub-mm, this is "is the vacuum *this* one?" data layered on the theoretical
  "can it exist?" axioms — kept separable via the opt-in flag (the Empirical
  Swampland).
