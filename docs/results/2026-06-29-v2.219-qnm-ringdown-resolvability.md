# v2.219 - First-principles ringdown resolvability: turning a QNM deviation into a detector SNR

## Question

v2.218 quantified the parity-splitting noise floor and showed the source-backed axial R4 QNM
shift sits ~3.5e4x above it. The next question is OBSERVABILITY: at what gravitational-wave
ringdown signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) does a fractional QNM deviation -- the R4 isospectrality
breaking, or any beyond-GR shift -- actually become measurable? Answer it self-contained, with
no un-cached appendix and no external Fisher coefficients to manufacture.

## Method (fully derivable in-house)

A single ringdown mode is a damped sinusoid `h(t) = A e^{-t/tau} cos(omega t + phi)`, `t >= 0`,
with `omega = omega_R`, `tau = 1/|omega_I|`, quality factor `Q = omega_R tau / 2`. The Fisher
matrix over `{ln A, phi, omega, tau}` (amplitude and phase marginalized as nuisance parameters)
is built by direct integration of the analytic waveform partials under a white-noise inner
product `(a|b) = integral_0^inf a b dt`. Two exact properties:

1. `sigma_theta ~ 1/rho` -- the standard Fisher 1/SNR scaling, exact here by construction.
2. **`R_f = rho sigma_f/f` and `R_tau = rho sigma_tau/tau` are SCALE-INVARIANT**, hence pure
   functions of `Q` alone (rescaling time `t -> t/a` maps `(omega, 1/tau) -> (a omega, a/tau)`
   with `Q` fixed). **Validated numerically** (`Q_scale_invariance_validated = True`).

## Result

For the `l=2 n=0` Schwarzschild gravitational ringdown (`Q = 2.10`):

```text
rho * sigma_f/f    = 0.554       (universal resolvability coefficient R_f)
rho * sigma_tau/tau = 2.001       (R_tau)
```

Both are O(1), consistent with the published Berti-Cardoso-Will ringdown Fisher analysis. A
fractional QNM frequency deviation `delta` is resolvable at 5 sigma when `rho > 5 R_f / delta`:

```text
delta = 1%   ->  rho_crit ~ 277
delta = 5%   ->  rho_crit ~ 55
delta = 10%  ->  rho_crit ~ 28
```

## Application: the R4 isospectrality reach

The source-backed axial R4 correction (v2.215) gives a fractional frequency deviation
`|Delta omega_R|/omega_R = 8.52` per unit `eta_2`. A ringdown of SNR `rho` therefore resolves
the R4-induced shift (5 sigma) for `eta_2 > 0.325 / rho`:

```text
rho ~ 8   (current single event)   ->  eta_2 reach ~ 0.041   (edge of the perturbative regime)
rho ~ 30                            ->  eta_2 reach ~ 0.011
rho ~ 100 (3G / LISA-era)           ->  eta_2 reach ~ 0.0032  (comfortably perturbative)
```

So isospectrality-resolved R4 ringdown bounds sharpen by ~an order of magnitude with
next-generation SNR -- and current single events sit right at the edge of where the linear
treatment is valid, quantifying *why* a confident single-event R4 ringdown bound is hard today.

## Honest scope (negatives preserved)

Idealized estimate: SINGLE mode, WHITE noise, ringdown-starts-at-`t=0` step (some spectral
leakage), no detector PSD coloring, no overtone/mode mixing. `R_f(Q)` is an O(1)
order-of-magnitude resolvability coefficient (consistent with BCW), NOT a detector-specific
forecast. The `eta_2` reach additionally carries the v2.215 `eta_2` normalization caveat (the
`(r_g/r)^10` prefactor convention) and the axial-only limitation -- the full isospectrality
splitting needs the un-sourceable polar correction (v2.218). Parity-odd `g_R4_c3` stays dark
(v2.209).

## What this establishes

- A self-contained, validated ringdown-resolvability tool: `R_f(Q)`, `R_tau(Q)`, and
  `rho_crit(delta)`, derived from first principles with an exact internal cross-check
  (scale-invariance / function-of-Q-only), reproducing the known O(1) BCW coefficients.
- It converts the abstract v2.218 QNM-deviation result into a concrete detector statement, and
  it honestly bounds the present-day R4 ringdown reach (current single-event SNR sits at the
  edge of perturbative validity; next-generation SNR gains ~1 order of magnitude).
