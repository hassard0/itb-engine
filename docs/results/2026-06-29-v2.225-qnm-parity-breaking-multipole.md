# v2.225 - Multipole robustness: is the isospectrality-breaking response physical?

## Question

v2.224 found the axial-vs-polar QNM response to a `(r_H/r)^j` deformation diverges monotonically
with `j`, reaching ~95% at `j=10` (where R4 lives) for the `l=2` mode. But that used the WKB solver
at `l=2`, where the WKB systematic is largest (v2.218: `l=2` isospectrality residual ~2e-4 vs `l=3`
~1e-6). **Is the striking breaking curve physical, or a low-`l` WKB artifact?**

## Result

Re-running the breaking across multipoles `l=2,3,4` (WKB becomes progressively more accurate):

```text
        j=2     j=6     j=10     monotonic in j
 l=2   12.3%   37.1%   95.3%        yes
 l=3    2.3%    6.8%   12.2%        yes
 l=4    0.8%    2.2%    3.8%        yes
        decreasing in l at every j:  yes
```

Both physical predictions hold:

1. **Monotonic in `j` at EVERY `l`** -- near-horizon deformations break isospectrality more,
   universally.
2. **Decreasing with `l`** -- at high `l` both potentials are dominated by the common centrifugal
   term `l(l+1) f / r^2` while the parity-distinguishing terms are `l`-independent, so the
   fractional potential difference (hence the parity-response difference) shrinks.

**The v2.224 result is physical, not a low-`l` WKB artifact:** the clean monotonic-in-`j` structure
PERSISTS at the WKB-accurate `l=3` and `l=4` (where the WKB error is ~2 orders of magnitude below
`l=2`). A pure WKB artifact would not reproduce that clean structure where WKB is accurate.

**Observational bonus:** the breaking is concentrated at the LOWEST multipole `l=2`, which is also
the LOUDEST ringdown mode -- so the dominant observable mode is the one most sensitive to
isospectrality breaking, and the R4 sweet spot (`l=2`, `j=10`) is near-maximal.

## What this establishes

Confirms the v2.224 isospectrality-breaking response as a physical effect (not a solver artifact)
by demonstrating its trend survives where the solver is most accurate, and adds the physically
coherent `l`-dependence (breaking concentrated at low `l`, consistent with the potentials
converging at high `l`). Together with v2.224 this gives a complete, in-house, self-consistent
picture of which operators (high-`j`, near-horizon) and which modes (low-`l`, loud) the
isospectrality discriminator is sensitive to -- and places R4 at the sweet spot of both.

## Honest scope

The TREND (monotonic in `j`, decreasing in `l`, consistent with potential convergence) is robust;
its persistence at the WKB-accurate `l=3,4` is the evidence it is physical. Absolute per-`(l,j)`
values still carry the v2.212 WKB-sensitivity caveat (worst at `l=2`), so the precise `l=2` `j=10`
number (~95%) is qualitative, not claim-grade -- the robust claim is the structure. The
`l`-decrease is real physics (the potentials converge), NOT merely improving WKB accuracy. Still
establishes the response structure, not the absolute R4 splitting magnitude (un-sourceable polar
`delta_V` profile). Parity-odd `g_R4_c3` stays dark (v2.209).
