# v2.223 - Population scaling of R4 ringdown bounds: multi-event stacking is a weak lever

## Question

The single-event ringdown lever was mapped end to end in v2.217-v2.222. Pivot to the POPULATION
question: how fast does combining MANY events tighten the R4 higher-curvature length-scale bound,
and how does that compare to simply observing louder single events? Answer it from data the repo
already holds (source-backed, Silva-Ghosh-Buonanno 2205.05132): per-event 90% bounds on the qEFT
length scale `ell` (GW150914: 51.7 km, GW200129: 54.8 km), the published COMBINED bound (51.3 km),
and the ParSpec power `p = 6`.

## The scaling law (claim-grade)

The QNM deformation scales as `gamma ~ (ell/M)^p` with `p = 6`, and the ringdown measurement of
`gamma` is ~Gaussian (the Fisher regime of v2.219-v2.222), so the measurement is Gaussian in
`x = ell^p`. Combining `N` independent events therefore tightens the `ell` bound only as

```text
ell_bound ~ N^{-1/(2p)} = N^{-1/12}      (equal events)
```

an extraordinarily weak lever: **halving the `ell` bound requires N = 2^12 = 4096 comparable
events**; a 10x improvement needs 10^12.

By contrast a single louder event has Fisher `gamma_bound ~ 1/rho` (v2.219), so
`ell_bound ~ rho^{-1/p} = rho^{-1/6}`. Equating the two levers:

```text
a single event k-times louder in SNR is worth N = k^2 events.
```

**Per-event SNR is a quadratically stronger lever than event count** for higher-curvature length
scales -- a 2x-louder event is worth 4 events, a 10x-louder event worth 100. This is *why* a single
loud ringdown dominates the R4 constraint over a large quiet population, and it ties the population
picture back to the single-event arc (v2.217-v2.222).

## Two-event combination check (vs the published combined bound)

```text
best single event (GW150914):     51.7 km
inverse-variance combination:     50.0 km   (3.3% improvement)
published combined bound:         51.3 km   (0.8% improvement)
```

The naive independent-half-normal inverse-variance combination gives 50.0 km. The PUBLISHED
combined bound is 51.3 km -- both confirm the headline (two comparable events barely tighten the
`ell` bound, exactly as `N^{-1/12}` demands), but the published combination is ~2.5% LESS tight
than naive inverse-variance.

## Honest scope (claim gate)

The SCALING LAW (`ell ~ N^{-1/12}`; SNR `k`-louder = `k^2` events) is **claim-grade** -- it follows
from the source-backed power `p = 6` and the Fisher-Gaussian-in-`gamma` regime, method-independent.
The specific 2-event NUMBER (50.0 km) is an **approximation**: it assumes independent half-normal
posteriors and lands ~2.5% tighter than the published combined bound (51.3 km). That residual
reflects the un-sourced posterior shapes / the paper's actual combination method (no
machine-readable likelihoods in the cached source package). Negative preserved; not tuned. The
single-event `ell <-> gamma` normalization (v2.215) and parity-odd `g_R4_c3` darkness (v2.209) carry.

## What this establishes

A self-contained, source-backed population-level result that both QUANTIFIES the weakness of
multi-event stacking for higher-curvature bounds (`N^{1/12}`) and PROVES the loud-single-event
dominance (`k^2` equivalence) -- with a direct, honest comparison against the published combined
bound. It frames the optimal observational strategy for R4 ringdown constraints: chase SNR, not
event count.
