# v2.347 - The parity sector is pinched between two birefringence observables (CMB vs GW): a near-term falsification handle

## Question

A fresh sector after the marginality arc (v2.344-346): a concrete falsifiable forecast. The parity coupling
`g_R2_parity` is the program's one data-pinned, distinguishing feature. Two *independent* birefringence
observables touch it -- do they agree, or do they squeeze it?

## Result (they nearly pinch it shut at realistic sensitivity)

- **CMB cosmic birefringence** (`beta = 0.34 +/- 0.09 deg`) pushes `g_R2_parity` **UP**: best-fit 0.10,
  2-sigma band `[0.0471, 0.1529]`. It is what makes parity nonzero.
- **GW birefringence** (LIGO/Virgo non-observation) bounds it from **ABOVE**. The engine loosens this to
  `<= 0.1` in the toy regime, but its OWN docstring states the real O3 strength is `|g_R2_parity| <~ 0.05`.

```text
  anomaly upper edge (default rho): 0.0783      constructed g_R2_parity: 0.06
  GW toy-loosened   (<= 0.1):  window [0.0471, 0.0783]  width 0.0312   0.06 inside?  True
  GW real-O3 estimate (<= 0.05): window [0.0471, 0.05]   width 0.0029   0.06 inside?  False
  falsification threshold: a GW bound below 0.0471 (the CMB floor) closes the window entirely
```

Under the toy bound the data-pinned window is fully open and 0.06 sits inside. Under the engine's own
real-O3 estimate the window collapses to the sliver `[0.0471, 0.05]` (width ~0.003) and the constructed
center **0.06 falls outside** -- the real GW bound, at face value, already disfavors the central parity
value and pins it to `~0.047-0.05`, right at the CMB floor. All 6/6 checks pass.

## What this establishes

The new theory's **most distinctive prediction is also its most testable**. The same parity coupling the CMB
hint *requires* (`>= 0.0471`) is what near-future GW-birefringence searches are closing in on *from the other
side* (`<~ 0.05` already). There is a sharp, near-term falsification threshold: if GW-birefringence
sensitivity tightens below the CMB lower edge `0.0471`, the two observables cross, the window **closes**, and
the parity-violating construction is falsified (given the CMB hint). This is a genuine "how to kill it"
handle on a real O4/O5 timescale -- and it sharpens the construction: if the real GW bound holds, the parity
coupling should be re-centered to `~0.048` (the CMB floor), not `0.06`.

## Honest scope

Both birefringence-to-coupling MAPS are toy normalizations with large uncertainty, and that matters acutely
here. The CMB map (`beta = 3.4 deg * g_R2_parity`) and the GW map (the `~0.05` real-O3 figure -- the
docstring calls it a *rough* translation) each carry O(1)-to-order-of-magnitude cutoff-scale and
propagation-distance uncertainty. So the *precise* exclusion of 0.06 and the exact sliver width 0.003 are
NOT robust; shift either map by an O(1) factor and the pinch loosens or tightens. What IS robust is the
**structure**: two independent parity observables push from opposite sides, the data-pinned window is
narrow, and the real (un-loosened) GW bound sits at or below the constructed value -- so the parity sector is
where the theory is most squeezed and most testable, and a modest GW improvement crosses the CMB floor and
falsifies it. The whole thing rests on the CMB hint being real (the ~3.6-sigma detection, v2.329 caveat); if
it is a systematic, the lower push vanishes and there is no pinch (and no parity requirement). The anomaly
upper edge uses the default `anomaly_rho` (v2.344: itself prefactor-dependent). Toy basis,
order-of-magnitude prefactors. A qualitative two-observable pinch with a concrete falsification direction,
not a precise exclusion.
