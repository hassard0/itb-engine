# v2.467 - the candidate and the H₀ tension: the canonical axion dark energy does not resolve it

Any dark-energy model must answer whether it helps or hurts the H₀ tension (Planck/CMB H₀ ~ 67 vs local/SH0ES
H₀ ~ 73, ~5σ). The candidate's dark energy is the model-independent axion (v2.458) — an **ultralight** (m ~ H₀ ~
10⁻³³ eV) **canonical** quintessence with **w ≥ −1** at all z. The two leading H₀-resolution mechanisms are both
closed to it:

```text
  mechanism                         resolves H0?   candidate can?
  phantom late DE (w < -1)             yes            NO   (canonical axion cannot cross w = -1)
  early dark energy (m ~ 1e-28 eV)     yes            NO   (candidate axion m ~ 1e-33 eV is too light -> late-only)
  canonical late quintessence (w>=-1)  no             yes  (what the candidate has; lowers CMB-inferred H0)
```

So the candidate's single model-independent axion **does not resolve the H₀ tension**: canonical late quintessence
(w ≥ −1) generically *lowers* the CMB-inferred H₀ (mildly worsening the tension, or at best neutral), and the axion
is too light to act as early dark energy. This is the same *"a canonical axion cannot cross w = −1"* limitation as
the DESI phantom-past tension (v2.454/v2.459), now against H₀ — so **H₀ is a third front aligned with the
dark-energy front**, testing the w ≥ −1 core prediction. A confirmed H₀-resolving *robust phantom* dark energy
would falsify the candidate's canonical-axion dark energy. 5/5 checks pass.

## The axiverse caveat

The heterotic UV completion (v2.434) naturally carries an **axiverse** — many axions across a wide mass range, not
just the model-independent one. So an EDE-scale axion (m ~ 10⁻²⁸ eV) for the H₀ tension is *available* in the same
string framework — just **not** as the model-independent (birefringence/dark-energy) axion. The candidate does not
*forbid* an H₀ resolution; it simply does not *provide* one from its single identified axion.

## Honest scope

A **physics-reasoning assessment** from standard cosmology (canonical scalars have w ≥ −1; H₀-resolution favors
phantom or EDE; the candidate's axion is ultralight late-time DE), not an engine computation of H₀. "Canonical
quintessence lowers/worsens H₀" is the generic expectation, but the exact shift depends on the full fit — so
"neutral-to-slightly-worse" is the honest range, not a computed ΔH₀. The axiverse EDE is a real *availability* in
heterotic compactifications but is **model-dependent and not a prediction** of the candidate. And H₀ is **not an
independent test** — it's the same phantom question (w ≥ −1) from the H₀ side, which is why it *reinforces* rather
than adds a new axis.
