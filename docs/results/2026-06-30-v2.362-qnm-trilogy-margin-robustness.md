# v2.362 - Does v2.361's margin-incomparability caveat invalidate v2.341's "which deep requirement binds" headline? (audited: no)

## Question

v2.361 discovered the engine's signed-distance margins are NOT cross-comparable (gw_speed's margin is ~5e-16
vs theoretical O(0.01-1)), so any margin-aggregating computation must restrict to comparably-scaled
constraints. v2.341 reached a headline by exactly such an aggregation -- comparing the worst margin across
three constraint groups (unitarity, causality, WGC). Is that conclusion a normalization artifact, or robust?

## Result (robust -- v2.341 survives)

```text
  unitarity  worst-margin 0.050   (range [0.050, 0.227])
  causality  worst-margin 0.122   (range [0.122, 0.238])
  wgc        worst-margin 0.091   (range [0.091, 0.440])

  all group margins in [0.050, 0.440],  max/min ratio 8.9   (vs ~1e13 for gw_speed)
  ordering: causality 0.122 > wgc 0.091 > unitarity 0.050   (flip ratio 2.46x)
```

All 5/5 checks pass. (1) None of v2.341's three groups contains a data / pathological-scale constraint --
they are all theoretical positivity/causality/swampland conditions. (2) Their margins are comparably scaled:
every group margin lies in `[0.050, 0.440]`, a max/min ratio of **8.9**, vs the `~1e13` ratio gw_speed would
introduce. (3) v2.341's ordering holds at the center -- causality loosest, unitarity tightest -- the
"causality has headroom, unitarity+WGC bind" conclusion, robust to a **2.46x** rescaling (far above O(1)
prefactor noise).

## What this establishes

v2.341's deep-requirement-binding headline is **not** an artifact of the incomparable-margin issue -- it is a
genuine comparison among comparably-scaled theoretical constraints. This closes the v2.361 -> v2.341
implication loop honestly: the newly-found caveat is real, but it does not *reach* v2.341 (which never
included the pathological-scale data constraints in its aggregation). The lesson generalizes -- the program's
margin-aggregating headlines should be checked against the v2.361 caveat, and here the check passes.

## Honest scope

This audits v2.341 at the CONSTRUCTED POINT; v2.341's actual conclusion was family-level (worst over ~2100
samples). The scale-comparability being audited is a per-constraint property (independent of the point), so
checking it at the center suffices to establish that the family-level comparison is also between comparable
scales -- but the exact family worst-margins are in v2.341, not re-derived here. "Comparably scaled" means
within a factor of ~tens, not identical; the ordering's robustness is quantified by the 2.46x flip ratio (a
>tens-fold per-constraint rescaling, which only the excluded data constraints have, could still matter; O(1)
prefactor noise cannot). The 50x and 1.5x thresholds are conventional. Conditional on the same toy basis +
screened stack. Toy basis, O(1) prefactors.
