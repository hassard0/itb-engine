# v2.50 - Weyl/g8 discriminator frontier: geometry confirmed, not yet a claim

## Question

v2.49 kept the birefringence/data-driven route alive, but still one-observable dominated. The next non-tower route is the full-basis frontier identified in v2.02/v2.12: the Weyl-squared coupling `g_C` and the high-moment matter coefficient `g_8`.

This audit asks whether `g_C`, `g_8`, `PCA:PC1`, and `PCA:PC2` can shrink the consistent island and separate named frameworks without pretending that internal PCA cuts are external measurements.

## Audit Basis

Artifact: `experiments/results/v2.50/weyl_g8_discriminator_frontier.json`

- Coefficient order: `g_4`, `g_6`, `g_8`, `g_R2`, `g_R3`, `g_C`, `g_R2_parity`, `g_R3_parity`
- Sampling slice: parity-even, with `g_R2_parity = g_R3_parity = 0`
- Stack: `build_stack(bnossw_mean="geometric", rfc_form="convex_hull")`
- Framework feasibility basis: full stack, not the reference-feasible tower subset
- PCA basis: raw covariance over sampled survivor vectors
- PC orientation: signs flipped so the largest absolute loading is positive

The audit records each framework's engine-scope verdict and whether its `g_C` is native or portrait-derived from `g_R2`.

## Result

The main run used 120,000 parity-even samples and found 680 full-stack survivors, for survival fraction 0.00567. Raw PCA effective dimension is 3.20.

| axis | dominant coefficient | island extent | key loading |
|---|---|---:|---|
| `PCA:PC1` | `g_C` | 0.1483 | `g_C = 0.806` |
| `single:g_C` | `g_C` | 0.1334 | `g_C = 1.0` |
| `PCA:PC2` | `g_8` | 0.1160 | `g_8 = 0.888` |
| `single:g_8` | `g_8` | 0.1120 | `g_8 = 1.0` |

Top single directions are:

```text
single:g_C
single:g_8
```

The seed jackknife used five 20,000-sample resamples. All five preserved the same geometry: top singles `single:g_C`, `single:g_8`, PC1 dominated by `g_C`, and PC2 dominated by `g_8`. Robustness pass fraction: 1.0 against the 0.8 acceptance bar.

## Cut Sensitivity

The audit separates arbitrary internal shrinkage cuts from favored-safe tangent cuts. Quantile cuts can remove many island points, but they can also exclude `discovered_data_driven`, so they are not claimable measurements.

Favored-safe tangent examples:

- `single:g_C` tangent at `discovered_data_driven`: removes 30.6% of the island, excludes only frameworks already full-stack excluded.
- `single:g_8` tangent at `discovered_data_driven`: removes 46.0% of the island and excludes `discovered_high_g8` plus already-excluded `horava_lifshitz`.
- `PCA:PC2` tangent at `discovered_data_driven`: removes 29.0% of the island and also isolates `discovered_high_g8`.

This is useful experiment-design information, not a discovery. The cuts are internal upper bounds on projections, not sourced external measurements.

## Interpretation

The non-tower frontier is now cleaner: after the one-observable birefringence audit, the next independent geometry is indeed the `g_C` / `g_8` plane. This is a stable island-geometry result, and it gives a concrete next target for sourceability work.

It still does not solve quantum gravity. A framework-level discriminator would require an external, source-backed measurement or bound for `g_C` or `g_8`, not just internal PCA geometry or toy observable maps.
