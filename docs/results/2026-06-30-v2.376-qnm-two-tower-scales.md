# v2.376 - SWING (attacked, survives): the theory is string-like in BOTH sectors -- two infinite towers, gravity harder than matter

## Question

v2.375 showed the curvature sector is an infinite log-convex tower. The matter sector (`g_4, g_6, g_8`) is
*also* a log-convex moment sequence (its dispersion tower `g_6^2 <= g_4 g_8`, v2.343), so it too generates an
infinite tower. How do the two towers' decay rates compare -- and is the comparison robust, or an artifact?

## Result (two towers, gravity harder -- and the attack survives)

Each sector's within-sector geometric decay ratio: `r_matter = g_6/g_4`, `r_curv = g_R3/g_R2`.

```text
  constructed:  r_matter = g_6/g_4 = 0.756,   r_curv = g_R3/g_R2 = 0.466   (curvature decays FASTER)

  attack: r_matter = 0.756 equals the dispersion ratio only because g_6 = g_8 at the center -- artifact?
  family: r_matter mean 0.718 [0.456, 0.995],   r_curv mean 0.397 [0.000, 0.833]
          curvature decays faster (r_curv < r_matter) in 89% of the feasible family
  scale separation r_curv/r_matter ~ 0.57 (mean) / 0.62 (constructed)
```

All 5/5 checks pass. The curvature tower decays faster than the matter tower in **89%** of the feasible family
-- not just at the `g_6 = g_8` center -- so "gravity harder than matter" is a **genuine structural feature,
not an artifact**.

## What this establishes

"String-like in two senses" (v2.342: closest to string in coupling space; UV string-like by unitarity+
causality) sharpens to **"string-like in both SECTORS as infinite towers, at different scales"**: the matter
and gravitational sectors both have infinitely many higher-derivative corrections (log-convex towers, v2.375),
but the gravitational tower is systematically **harder** -- its higher-curvature operators are more suppressed
order-by-order (a higher effective cutoff), with a dimensionless scale separation `r_curv/r_matter ~ 0.6`. The
two towers are distinct (confirming v2.368's refutation of an identical-spectrum shared tower), with gravity
the more strongly suppressed. Physically: the graviton's higher-curvature corrections converge faster than
matter's higher-derivative ones.

## Honest scope

Both towers being log-convex moment sequences is rigorous given the dispersive representation (v2.261/v2.369);
the within-sector decay ratios (`g_6/g_4`, `g_R3/g_R2`) are clean dimensionless decay rates of each sector's
own geometric floor. "Curvature harder than matter" is robust (89% of the family -- the attack survives), but
it COMPARES ratios across two sectors whose couplings carry different dimensions/normalizations in the
engine's O(1)-normalized toy basis, so reading it as a literal physical scale separation inherits that caveat
-- the robust content is the ORDERING (gravity faster-decaying), not the precise 0.57-0.62 number. The ~11%
where curvature decays slower are the family edge (small `g_R2` / large `g_R3`, near the CEMZ boundary).
`g_R5+`/`g_10+` are not engine couplings (the towers are the assumed moment structure, v2.375 caveat). The
family is a seeded random walk (sampled). Toy basis for the numbers, rigorous-given-dispersive-structure for
the tower existence, robust for the ordering.
