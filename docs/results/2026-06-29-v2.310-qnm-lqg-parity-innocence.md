# v2.310 - Is lqg the consistency boundary BECAUSE of its parity violation? The engine says no

## Question

lqg_induced is repeatedly flagged as the engine's consistency boundary (v2.262, v2.299) AND it is the
ONLY parity-violating framework (g_R2_parity = 0.08, g_R3_parity = 0.04; all others exactly parity-even).
The natural hypothesis: lqg's parity sector is what makes it infeasible. Is it?

## Result (hypothesis refuted by the engine's own verdict)

No. Three probes, all the engine's literal output on the full 38-constraint stack:

**1. Toggle.** lqg fails 6 constraints; setting `g_R2_parity = g_R3_parity = 0` and re-checking leaves
that set IDENTICAL:

```text
  full lqg:    bnossw_monogamy, cft_flat_space_bound, complexity_cutoff,
               cross_sector_efthedron, graviton_forward_positivity, repulsive_force_conjecture
  parity OFF:  (identical -- the same 6)
  parity-sensitive constraints at lqg: none
```

All 6 failures are CP-even (matter/curvature magnitude conditions); none reads the parity couplings. The
parity sector is innocent of lqg's infeasibility.

**2. Headroom.** lqg's parity violation is itself consistent but MARGINAL: at its actual value
(g_R2_parity = 0.08) zero parity-specific constraints fire, but scaling parity up by only ~10%
(factor 1.1) trips the first one (`generalized_anomaly_inflow`; `ligo_birefringence_bound` follows at
1.3). So lqg sits *just* inside the carved parity window -- marginal on parity as it is on everything
else -- but parity is not among its actual failures.

**3. Magnitude.** The CP-even tension is not a simple magnitude problem either: uniformly shrinking lqg's
ordinary couplings does NOT heal the failures -- it adds one (6 -> 7 at scale 0.8):

```text
  CP-even scale   1.0   0.9   0.8   0.7   0.6   0.5
  n_fail           6     6     7     7     7     7
```

So the tension is structural in lqg's coupling RATIOS, not the overall scale and not the parity. All 7/7
checks pass.

## What this establishes

lqg's boundary status and its much-remarked parity violation are INDEPENDENT facts. Its infeasibility is a
CP-even, shape-of-the-couplings effect (6 failures, parity-toggle-invariant, not cured by rescaling); its
parity violation is a consistent, sub-threshold feature riding along (marginally inside the parity
window). The natural "parity causes lqg's problem" story is wrong -- a clean example of the engine
refuting a plausible hypothesis, and of separating two properties that a framework happens to carry at
once.

## Honest scope

Every verdict here is the engine's literal output on the full 38-constraint stack (no schematic mapping):
the toggle, the headroom multiplier, and the magnitude scan are direct `check()` results. The headroom
multiplier and which parity constraint bites first depend on the O(1) parity-constraint prefactors, so the
exact factor (1.1) is convention-dependent -- the robust content is STRUCTURAL: (i) lqg's failures are
CP-even and parity-toggle-invariant; (ii) lqg's parity is inside the carved parity window (headroom > 1);
(iii) uniform downscaling does not heal the CP-even failures. The "ratios not magnitude" reading is
supported by the downscaling probe but not exhaustively diagnosed (the precise binding combination of
couplings is not isolated here). lqg is the only parity-violating framework, so the toggle is a genuine
no-op for the others. Toy basis, O(1) prefactors.
