what it measures, the candidate's prediction, the rival it distinguishes (v2.420), the **rigor tier** of the
prediction, and the timeline. A decision map ("so what would actually settle this?"), not new physics.

All 5/5 checks pass.

## What this establishes

The candidate is falsifiable on **two independent fronts** atop a rigorous backbone: **parity** (CMB
birefringence now, GW birefringence later — if both null, the parity-conserving rival B wins) and **matter**
(CMB-S4 inflation — if clean slow-roll, matter dominance breaks). Crucially it is **not hostage to one
measurement**: the parity question has redundant probes in *different sectors* (photon vs graviton), and the
matter test is orthogonal to parity. The rigorous backbone (LQG excluded, extreme archetypes excluded) is already
settled, zero toy input. So "how will we know?" has a clean answer — the two fronts + timelines above.

## Honest scope

A consolidation / decision map, not new physics — it assembles predictions from prior cycles
(v2.387/395/408/418/420) with rigor tiers attached; the only live computation is re-confirming the rigorous LQG
exclusion as the anchor. Timelines are approximate experiment horizons. The tiers are the crux: the parity *sign*
and the rigorous ceiling are source-exact, but every *magnitude* (β, GW-birefringence size, g_4's inflationary
imprint) rests on toy observable maps — so "predicts nonzero β/chirality" is robust while predicted *sizes* are
order-of-magnitude. The birefringence "hint" is ~3.6σ (v2.329), not a detection. The GW-vs-CMB single-origin test
assumes the two parity signals share an origin (single-axion, v1.81) — which is what the comparison would test.
