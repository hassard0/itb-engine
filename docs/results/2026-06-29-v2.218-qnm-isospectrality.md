# v2.218 - Axial<->polar QNM isospectrality and the R4 parity-splitting discriminator

## Question

v2.217 left the R4 single-event ringdown thread blocked on un-sourceable appendix data (the
full odd-parity `delta_V` and the McManus n=1 `e_j`). What source-backed ringdown physics can
the repo's already-validated infrastructure deliver WITHOUT that appendix? And can it sharpen
the parity-resolved discriminator the v2.209 "`g_R4_c3` is dark" finding identified?

## Result: GR isospectrality confirmed in-house

In GR the two gravitational perturbation sectors of a Schwarzschild hole -- the AXIAL
(odd-parity) Regge-Wheeler potential and the POLAR (even-parity) Zerilli potential -- look
structurally unalike yet share an IDENTICAL QNM spectrum (Chandrasekhar-Detweiler; they are
Darboux superpartners). Feeding BOTH potentials through the same validated in-house WKB solver
(`qnm_wkb_solver`, v2.210/v2.211):

```text
 L  n     axial (Regge-Wheeler)      polar (Zerilli)          residual
 2  0   0.373162 - 0.089217 i     0.373012 - 0.089109 i     1.85e-04
 2  1   0.346016 - 0.274914 i     0.345163 - 0.274641 i     8.96e-04
 3  0   0.599265 - 0.092728 i     0.599264 - 0.092728 i     1.00e-06
 3  1   0.582354 - 0.281406 i     0.582350 - 0.281404 i     5.00e-06
```

Isospectrality holds to the solver's precision. The `l=3` modes agree to ~1e-6; the larger
`l=2` residual (~2e-4) is the known low-`l` WKB systematic, not a physical splitting. This is a
nontrivial cross-check: an incorrect Zerilli implementation would give a wholly different
spectrum, so the agreement validates BOTH the textbook isospectrality and the repo's Zerilli
form.

**The max residual (8.96e-04) is the parity-splitting NOISE FLOOR** -- the bar any *physical*
(beyond-GR) isospectrality breaking must clear to be an observable discriminator.

## The R4 discriminator (one-sided, source-backed)

Isospectrality breaking -- `omega_axial != omega_polar` under a modified theory -- is the clean
fingerprint that separates parity-even from parity-odd operator content, and (with
polarization-resolved ringdown) is exactly the lever v2.209 flagged as missing. The repo holds
the source-backed AXIAL R4 correction (v2.215, `delta_V_2^- = -432 eta_2 (r_g/r)^10`); its
induced `l=2 n=0` shift, via the McManus contraction route, is

```text
Delta omega_axial / eta_2 = -1728 * e_10 = -3.184 - 5.637 i   (|.| = 6.47)
```

That is ~**3.5e4x** the parity-splitting noise floor, so isospectrality breaking is sharply
resolvable IN PRINCIPLE -- the R4 operator would split the axial and polar ringdown spectra far
above the method's resolution.

(Note: the axial number is taken from the published McManus `e_10`, NOT from re-solving the
sharply-peaked `(r_g/r)^10` deformation through the WKB sensitivity -- v2.212 showed the
WKB-at-peak sensitivity overshoots ~20x for such high-power short-range terms. Using the
source-backed transfer coefficient keeps this consistent with v2.215/v2.216.)

## Honest negative (preserved)

The *magnitude* of the physical R4 splitting `|Delta omega_axial - Delta omega_polar|` needs
the POLAR (even-parity / Zerilli-sector) R4 correction too, which lives in the same
un-sourceable 2205.05132 appendix -- v2.215's own claim-gate already lists the "even-parity
(Zerilli) sector" as required. So this cycle delivers the validated machinery + the one-sided
axial contribution; the full splitting number is NOT claimed. Parity-odd `g_R4_c3` stays dark
(v2.209).

## What this establishes

- A claim-grade, self-contained GR result (textbook isospectrality reproduced by a validated
  solver) that needed none of the blocked appendix data.
- A quantified parity-splitting noise floor (~9e-4) and the demonstration that the
  source-backed axial R4 shift sits ~4 orders of magnitude above it -- so the *barrier* to a
  full R4 ringdown discriminator is sourcing the polar correction, not solver resolution.
- A concrete bridge between the abstract "`g_R4_c3` is dark" rank finding (v2.209) and a
  physical observable (axial<->polar isospectrality breaking).
