# v2.346 - How marginal is the constructed theory under JOINT O(1) prefactor variation?

## Question

v2.345 swept each of the 11 knife-edge prefactors one-at-a-time (OAT) and found the constructed theory
feasible across >=78% of every band individually -- but flagged that OAT cannot see JOINT excursions, where
several prefactors sit at their adverse edges together. This closes that gap with a Monte Carlo over the
full 11-dimensional prefactor box (each prefactor uniform over its declared factor-of-~2 band).

## Result (joint feasibility 41%, vs >=78% per single axis -- the OAT view was optimistic)

```text
  JOINT feasible fraction:       41.2%   (1200 samples over the 11-D declared box)
  OAT-independence estimate:     37.8%   (product of v2.345 per-axis feasible fractions)
  min single-axis OAT fraction:  77.8%   (the optimistic single-axis view)

  top violators under joint variation:
    bnossw_monogamy              23.2%
    scalar_wgc                   22.5%
    matter_s3_positivity         14.2%
    complexity_cutoff            10.2%
    generalized_anomaly_inflow    7.4%
    cft_flat_space_bound          6.0%
```

Under joint uniform variation, the constructed theory stays feasible only **41%** of the time -- far below
the **>=78%-per-axis** figure OAT reported, and close to the **37.8%** an independent-breaks model predicts.
So the v2.345 OAT view *was* optimistic, exactly as its caveat warned.

## What this establishes

The constructed point is **genuinely marginal under joint O(1) ignorance**: each single prefactor leaves
>=78% headroom, but varying all of them together puts the point *outside* the feasible region most of the
time (infeasible ~59%). The joint fraction (41%) sitting just *above* the independence product (38%) means
the six load-bearing breaks are **roughly independent** -- they act through six *distinct* constraints
(BNOSSW monogamy, scalar WGC, matter s^3 positivity, the complexity cutoff, anomaly inflow, the CFT bound)
rather than one common mode, so they compound multiplicatively rather than reinforcing. The point is still a
**genuine feasible point** (it is feasible at canonical, and ~41% of the whole declared box is feasible) --
not a knife-edge artifact -- but its feasibility has a thin *joint* cushion, not a fat interior one. The
honest conclusion: the result's robust content is its **structure** (the point exists, beats the community
frameworks, its qualitative features) rather than the precise feasibility of the single Chebyshev-center
point under adversarial joint prefactor choices.

## Honest scope

The joint fraction depends on the **sampling measure** -- uniform-independent over each declared band is a
convention; correlated priors (the prefactors are not physically independent) or different band widths would
move the number. The bands are the engine's own factor-of-~2 conventions (`PLAUSIBLE_RANGES`), not published
uncertainties, so this is a statement about the engine's *declared ignorance*, not a physical probability.
It is a Monte Carlo estimate (finite n, one seed; ~1-2% sampling error at n=1200). "Feasible" is the binary
all-constraints-satisfied for the single constructed point under the toy-basis stack with the 4 data
constraints (incl. the cosmic-birefringence hint, v2.329 caveat). The OAT-independence estimate reuses the
coarse 9-point grid fractions from v2.345. Robust content: joint feasibility is markedly below the
single-axis OAT figure and near the independence product, so the point is marginal (not robust) under joint
O(1) variation, and the load-bearing breaks act through distinct constraints. Toy basis, O(1) prefactors.
