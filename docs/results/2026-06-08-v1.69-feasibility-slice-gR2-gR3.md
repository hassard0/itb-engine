# v1.69 — The allowed region, seen directly: a 2-D feasibility slice in the graviton-curvature plane

**Date:** 2026-06-08
**Compute:** Vulcan (16-core); figure rendered headless.

## What this adds

Every prior cycle reported feasibility as a *number* (survival fraction, a
binding constraint, a verdict). v1.69 makes the engine's allowed region **a
shape you can see**. We sweep the two graviton-curvature couplings
— g_R2 (leading, R²) on x and g_R3 (cubic, R³) on y — over [0, 0.5]², holding
the matter sector fixed at a string-like survivor point (g_4=0.5, g_6=0.4,
g_8=0.4, parity off). At each of 121×121 = 14 641 cells we evaluate the
**corrected** stack (geometric BNOSSW + convex-hull RFC, 35 constraints) and
color the cell either *feasible* or by its **binding constraint** — the single
most-violated bound there.

`experiments/slice_gR2_gR3.py`; figure `slice_gR2_gR3.png`.

## The picture

- **Feasible region = 11.7%** of the slice (1718 / 14 641 cells): a compact
  green wedge at small-to-moderate g_R2 with g_R3 ≲ 0.2. The allowed set is not
  a box — it is a **triangle whose hypotenuse is graviton forward-positivity**:
  as g_R2 grows, the ceiling on g_R3 falls.

- **Two walls do almost all the work.** Of the 12 919 infeasible cells:
  - `graviton_forward_positivity` binds **5367** — the entire upper band. This
    is the dispersion bound coupling leading and cubic curvature; it is what
    tilts the feasible region into a wedge.
  - `cft_flat_space_bound` binds **4777** — the whole upper-right. Large g_R2
    *and* large g_R3 together violate the CFT→flat-space mapping bound.
  - `swampland_distance_conjecture` (1185) and `scalar_wgc` (1100) wall off the
    **axes** (very small g_R2 or g_R3) and the high-g_R2 strip.
  - `repulsive_force_conjecture` (494) takes only the far-right g_R2 ≳ 0.45
    corner (purple). The convex-hull RFC is a *mild* constraint here — exactly
    the v1.62 lesson that the original `matter_product` RFC was a spurious
    near-universal excluder; corrected, it barely bites.

## Where the frameworks land

| framework | (g_R2, g_R3) | in this slice |
|---|---|---|
| asymptotic-safety | (0.15, 0.10) | ✓ feasible |
| string-EFT | (0.20, 0.15) | ✓ feasible |
| CDT | (0.22, 0.15) | ✓ feasible |
| LQG-induced | (0.30, 0.30) | ✗ — falls in the CFT-flat-space wall |

Three of four sit inside the green wedge. **LQG is the outlier**, and the
picture says *why*: its large cubic coupling g_R3 = 0.30 paired with g_R2 = 0.30
lands deep in the `cft_flat_space_bound` region. The wedge's hypotenuse passes
just below string/CDT and well below LQG.

## Honest caveats

1. **This is a 2-D slice, not the verdict.** LQG is plotted at its native
   (g_R2, g_R3) but on a *string-like matter background* (g_4=0.5), not its own
   (g_4=0.6, g_6=0.45). LQG's full-dimensional survival is decided elsewhere;
   here it shows only that LQG's curvature sector is incompatible with a
   string-like matter sector — a statement about the *joint* point, as it should
   be. The figure is a cross-section through a 7-D feasible body, chosen to make
   the dominant graviton physics legible.

2. **The wedge shape is the robust content.** Exact areas move with the toy
   prefactors (forward-positivity c=1.2, CFT α=0.5), but the *qualitative*
   structure — a forward-positivity hypotenuse, a CFT-flat-space ceiling on the
   joint upper-right, swampland walls hugging the axes, RFC mild — survived the
   v1.62 correction and is what the picture is for.

3. The matter point and parity=0 are fixed choices; other slices (e.g. parity
   on, or LQG-native matter) give different cross-sections. This one is the
   string-survivor reference slice.

## Why it matters

The realism program has been about *which conclusions survive the toy numbers*.
This cycle answers a complementary question — **what does the surviving allowed
region look like** — and the answer is a clean, falsifiable geometric statement:
the graviton-curvature sector of a string-like matter theory is confined to a
forward-positivity wedge with a CFT ceiling, and the consistent quantum-gravity
candidates cluster along its lower edge while LQG's cubic coupling pushes it out.
