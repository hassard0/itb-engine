## Source-backed result: overtones are the dominant R4 lever

Pivoting to data the repo ALREADY holds -- the qEFT/ParSpec QNM deformation coefficients for
BOTH overtones (Silva-Ghosh-Buonanno, 2205.05132) -- this cycle computes the complex QNM
shift per unit ParSpec coupling `gamma` for the `l=2` fundamental and first overtone, with the
v2.216 convention `d(omega_R)/d(gamma) = omega_R^0 dwq`, `d(omega_I)/d(gamma) = -omega_I^0 dtq`:

```text
                 dwq        dtq        Delta omega / gamma        |Delta omega / gamma|
n=0 fundamental  -0.2114    -0.6070    -0.0790 - 0.0540 i         0.0957
n=1 overtone     -1.5263   171.35      -0.5292 + 46.94 i         46.94
```

Three source-backed findings:

1. **~491x enhancement.** The first overtone's complex QNM shift per unit coupling is ~491x
   larger than the fundamental's (damping-coefficient ratio alone is 282x). The R4 constraint
   is **overtone-dominated**.

2. **Opposite-sign damping response.** Under R4 the fundamental decays *faster*
   (`Delta omega_I < 0`) while the first overtone decays *much slower* (`Delta omega_I > 0`) --
   a qualitative spectral fingerprint, not just a magnitude difference.

3. **Physically expected.** Overtones decay fast and are weighted toward the near-horizon /
   potential-peak region, exactly where the higher-curvature `(r/r)`-suppressed R4 correction
   concentrates. This identifies overtone-resolved ringdown spectroscopy as the powerful probe
   of higher-curvature gravity (cf the active overtone literature, e.g. arXiv:2512.22728).

## Perturbative caveat (honest)

`dtq_1 = 171.35` is very large (O(170)): the ParSpec linear-in-`gamma` expansion is only valid
for `gamma << 1/170 ~ 6e-3`, so the overtone bound is perturbatively **delicate** even as it
dominates. The precise n=1 number should be treated cautiously; the QUALITATIVE result
(overtones vastly more sensitive, opposite damping sign) is robust.
