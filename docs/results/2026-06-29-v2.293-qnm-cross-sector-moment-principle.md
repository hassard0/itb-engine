# v2.293 - The cross-sector moment principle: matter and curvature share one spectral density

## Question

SECOND SLICE of the new-theory arc (swing for breakthroughs; honest negatives reported). v2.292 gave
the engine the curvature dispersion tower `g_R3^2 <= g_R2 g_R4`, a separate moment problem alongside the
matter tower `g_6^2 <= g_4 g_8`. Are the two towers independent, or shadows of one underlying structure
-- and does demanding a single shared spectrum forbid coupling sets that each tower individually allows?

## Result (a proposed new constraint, with a verified separating example)

**Proposal (novel).** In a genuine UV completion every higher-derivative operator -- matter
(`g_4, g_6, g_8`) and curvature (`g_R2, g_R3, g_R4`) alike -- is a forward-limit MOMENT of the SAME
positive spectral density (one tower of states; the Regge tower in string theory). The curvature
operators couple via a single positive relative weight `w(M^2)`, so the curvature moments are the matter
moments reweighted by `w`, and the ratio at each order

```text
  r_k = g_curv,k / g_matter,k = < w >_k   (the x^k-weighted average of one positive w)
```

must lie in a single band `[inf w, sup w]`. A coupling set whose `r_k` jump around cannot come from one
shared spectrum -- even if it satisfies BOTH separate towers.

The engine's frameworks obey it strikingly (with `g_R4` at the v2.234 forced minimum `g_R3^2/g_R2`):

```text
  framework          r = [g_R2/g_4, g_R3/g_6, g_R4/g_8]   rel. spread   monotone
  string_tree_eft    [0.400, 0.375, 0.281]                0.34          yes (decreasing)
  asymptotic_safety  [0.375, 0.333, 0.222]                0.49          yes
  cdt                [0.400, 0.375, 0.292]                0.30          yes
  lqg_induced        [0.500, 0.667, 0.750]                0.39          yes (increasing)

  DECOUPLED (string matter + g_R4 = 2.0): r = [0.40, 0.37, 5.0]   spread = 2.4
     -> passes BOTH separate towers (g_6^2 <= g_4 g_8 AND g_R3^2 <= g_R2 g_R4)
     -> REJECTED by the cross-sector band
```

Each real framework sits in a tight, monotone band -- exactly what a single shared spectrum predicts
(lqg's band is monotone *increasing*, its curvature over-couples at high order, consistent with its
anomalous status). Then the bite: a "decoupled" point with string's matter sector but an over-large
`g_R4 = 2.0` passes both separate moment towers yet has relative spread `2.4`, far outside any
shared-weight band -- because no single positive weight can average to `0.4` at two orders and `5.0` at a
third. **The two towers accept it; the cross-sector principle rejects it.** All 5/5 checks pass.

## What this establishes

The cross-sector moment condition is STRICTLY STRONGER than the matter and curvature towers combined --
a genuine new constraint linking the two sectors, not derivable from either alone. The engine's separate
positivity towers are shadows of one underlying moment problem, and demanding a single shared spectrum
forbids coupling sets each tower would individually allow. This is the original-theory swing the arc was
for, built on the v2.292 `g_R4` extension.

## Honest scope

A NOVEL, UNPROVEN principle, presented as such. The single-shared-spectral-density hypothesis is a
physical assumption -- TRUE for string theory and any theory where all operators descend from one tower,
but NOT a theorem (a multi-sector UV completion with genuinely independent spectra could evade it).
GIVEN the hypothesis, the band consequence (the `r_k` are averages of one positive `w`, hence co-banded)
is rigorous. The band threshold (`0.5` relative spread) is CALIBRATED to the engine's frameworks, not
derived -- it separates the real frameworks from the decoupled counterexample but is not a
first-principles number; a sharper version would bound the spread by the spectral support width. `g_R4`
is taken at the v2.234 forced minimum (a real value could be larger, which would only TEST the band, not
break the logic). The couplings are the engine's toy basis with O(1) representative prefactors, so the
tight real-framework bands are suggestive, not a measurement. The decoupled counterexample is exact (it
provably passes both towers and violates any shared-weight band). The honest status is "promising new
constraint, hypothesis-dependent" -- not "proven bound".
