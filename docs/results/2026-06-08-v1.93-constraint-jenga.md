# v1.93 — Constraint Jenga: which consistency conditions are load-bearing, and which are redundant?

**Date:** 2026-06-08
**Compute:** Vulcan (16-core; 1.5M-sample bitmask cache).

---

## The question

The engine imposes 37 theoretical constraints. **How many actually do work?** We pull
each one out and measure how much the consistent island *grows* — the growth is that
constraint's **irreplaceability**. Redundant constraints (implied by the others) grow
the island by ~nothing.

## Method

Reusing the Gödel-test trick: sample the box once (1.5M), cache each point's
per-constraint satisfied-bitmask as a `uint64`, then "island volume without constraint
c" = count of samples whose bitmask is all-set-except-possibly-c = `((bits | (1<<c)) ==
full).sum()` — an instant bitwise op, so all 37 removals cost one sampling pass.

## Result 1 — a few constraints carry the island; most are redundant

Island V0 = 363 / 1.5M (0.024%). Ranked by irreplaceability (island growth on removal):

| rank | constraint | class | island growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **swampland_distance_conjecture** | C | **+664%** |
| 2 | **generalized_anomaly_inflow** | C | **+317%** |
| 3 | complexity_cutoff | C | +74% |
| 4 | t_hooft_anomaly_matching | C | +62% |
| 5 | dispersion_tower_g6_squared_bound | A | +56% |
| 6 | scalar_convexity_g6_vs_g4 | A | +52% |
| 7 | hofman_maldacena_wedge | A | +36% |
| 8 | bekenstein_tight | B | +24% |

- **The swampland and anomaly sectors carry the island.** The distance conjecture and
  anomaly inflow alone, removed, would grow the consistent region by ~7× and ~4× — they
  are by far the most load-bearing. The complexity cutoff, 't Hooft matching, the
  dispersion-tower bound, convexity, and the Hofman–Maldacena wedge form the next tier.
- **22 of 37 constraints are *redundant* in this basis** (≈0 growth — implied by the
  others): *all* the scalar-positivity moments (g_4, g_6, g_8), graviton-mixed and cubic
  positivity, spin-four, CEMZ causality, holographic subadditivity, BNOSSW monogamy,
  quantum focusing, the generalized second law, all four parity/helicity positivity
  bounds, the LIGO bounds, EFT-validity, WGC, RFC, and **`wald_entropy_positivity`** —
  *systematically confirming the v1.82 finding* that the BH-entropy/WGC condition is
  redundant here. The amplitude positivity *tower* is largely self-implied: the
  strongest moment subsumes the weaker ones.

So the engine's ~40-axiom edifice rests on a **~15-constraint load-bearing core**,
dominated by the swampland.

## Result 2 — framework exclusions are robust (no single constraint is decisive)

For each constraint we asked: which frameworks *flip* survival (fail-with → pass-without)
when it's removed — i.e. which constraint, if it turned out *wrong*, would change the
verdict on which quantum gravity survives? **The answer is none.** No single constraint
flips any framework: every excluded framework (LQG, Hořava, GFT, the discovered
parity-violating branch, …) fails **multiple** constraints at once. The exclusions are
**robust** — you cannot rescue any excluded theory by doubting one bound. (This makes
quantitative the earlier "LQG is *redundantly* disfavoured" result.)

## Honest scope

Redundancy is **basis/encoding-dependent**: a constraint redundant in this 8-coefficient
toy basis can become load-bearing in a fuller one — exactly what happened to the
a-theorem (v1.70, dormant) once the basis split Euler from Weyl² (v1.71). So the list of
"redundant" constraints is *"redundant given the current basis and the other axioms,"*
not "useless." The robust content is the **ranking** — the swampland sector does the
heavy lifting, the positivity tower is largely self-implied, and the exclusions are
multiply-redundant.

## Headline

The engine's 37 theoretical constraints rest on a **~15-constraint load-bearing core**
dominated by the **swampland distance conjecture and anomaly inflow** (removing either
grows the island several-fold), with **22 constraints redundant** in this basis
(including the entire scalar-positivity tower and the BH-entropy/WGC condition). And
**no single constraint is decisive for any framework's fate** — every excluded quantum
gravity is excluded by several conditions at once, so the verdicts are robust.
