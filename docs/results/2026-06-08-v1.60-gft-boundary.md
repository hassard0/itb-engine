## The scan

Group Field Theory's verdict was left hanging on its cubic curvature coupling.
Holding g_R2 = 0.28, we scan g_R3 so the ratio g_R2/g_R3 runs 1.0 → 2.0 and

## Verdict

**GFT crosses from excluded to viable at g_R2/g_R3 ≈ 1.30** (g_R3 ≲ 0.215). Below
that it fails — forward positivity at the LQG-like ratio 1.0, then the
CFT-flat-space bound at 1.1–1.2. So:

- **LQG-like GFT** (raw spin-foam-vertex cubic, ratio ≈ 1.0): robustly disfavoured
  (3%), exactly like LQG.
- **Condensate-GR-limit GFT** (Dr. M.'s ratio ≈ 1.5): **viable** (21%), joining the
  survivors — though at the lower end (its larger g_4 and parity content keep it
  below Causal Set's 43% and string's 66%).

GFT's fate therefore hinges on a **single condensate quantity**: how much the
mean-field/condensate dynamics suppress the effective cubic curvature coupling
relative to the quadratic. If the GFT condensate genuinely recovers a clean GR
limit (cubic suppressed, ratio ≳ 1.3) it survives; if it inherits the bare
spin-foam vertex enhancement (ratio ~ 1) it is disfavoured with LQG. This is a
concrete, calculable target for the GFT-cosmology community: compute the
effective g_R3/g_R2 of the condensate background.

## Why this is a good outcome

The engine converts a vague "is GFT like LQG?" into a sharp, falsifiable
condition on one effective coupling ratio (1.3) — and identifies *which*
constraint enforces it (forward positivity, the same bound that orders the whole
parity-conserving survivor set). The borderline is not a weakness; it is the
engine pointing at exactly the quantity a GFT practitioner should compute.

## Honest limitations

Representative GFT coefficients (g_4, g_6, g_8, parity fixed); the boundary ratio
1.3 is specific to those values and the corrected stack — different representative
matter/parity values shift it modestly. The robust content: there IS a sharp
cubic-suppression threshold near ratio 1.3, and GFT sits on the knife-edge between
the LQG-like and condensate-GR-limit interpretations.
