# v2.69 - GW parity Callister fixed-variable comparison: source-native sensitivity quantified

## Question

v2.68 converted the Callister variable-evolution samples into normalized
source-native histogram densities. This iteration asks whether those densities
can be compared to a fixed-rate grid posterior with a reproducible statistic,
without interpreting the comparison as PPV or engine evidence.

## Comparison

Reference files:

| role | file |
|---|---|
| fixed-rate grid | `fixed_rate_delayedSFR.hdf` |
| variable-evolution samples | `birefringence_variable_evolution.hdf` |

The comparison interpolates normalized one-dimensional posterior densities onto
their common source-native support and reports total variation distance,
Hellinger distance, and peak offset.

| coordinate | overlap | fixed peak | variable peak | peak offset | TV distance | Hellinger |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `kappa_D` vs `kappa_Dc` | `[-0.2, 0.2]` | `0.1021526419` | `0.0833659491` | `-0.0187866928` | `0.3187723937` | `0.4212740103` |
| `kappa_z` | `[-0.4, 0.4]` | `-0.0602739726` | `-0.0133072407` | `0.0469667319` | `0.3712304727` | `0.4368761662` |

These are release-internal sensitivity diagnostics. They quantify how much the
native posterior changes between one fixed-rate assumption and the
variable-evolution population model. They do not exclude a framework.

## Interpretation

The Callister branch now has enough source-native plumbing to compare public
posterior products against each other. The result is useful because it exposes
population-model sensitivity before any PPV or engine-axis mapping is attempted.
It is still not a quantum-gravity discriminator.

No `g_R2_parity`, `g_R3_parity`, or final PPV `beta_1` likelihood is produced.
