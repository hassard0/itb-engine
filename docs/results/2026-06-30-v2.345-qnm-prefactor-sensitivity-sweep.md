# v2.345 - Which of the engine's 11 O(1) prefactors is the constructed theory's feasibility sensitive to?

## Question

v2.344 audited ONE prefactor (`anomaly_rho`) against ONE result (the parity window). The honest global
question: of the engine's eleven knife-edge O(1) prefactors -- each with a declared factor-of-~2 ignorance
band (`experiments/stack.py` `PLAUSIBLE_RANGES`) -- how many does the constructed theory's overall
FEASIBILITY (all theoretical + 4 data constraints satisfied) actually depend on?

## Result (feasible-but-marginal: 5 slack, 6 load-bearing, every break an edge effect)

The constructed theory is feasible at canonical prefactors (no violations). Sweeping each prefactor across
its band one-at-a-time (others canonical):

```text
  SLACK : cemz_kappa, cubic_kappa, efthedron_alpha, graviton_fwd_c, rfc_gamma  (feasible across full band)
  LOAD  : anomaly_rho      [0.03,0.12]  break@0.03  -> generalized_anomaly_inflow
          bnossw_pref      [0.5, 2.0]   break@0.50  -> bnossw_monogamy
          cft_alpha        [0.25,1.0]   break@0.25  -> cft_flat_space_bound
          complexity_cmax  [1.0, 2.5]   break@1.00  -> complexity_cutoff
          matter_s3_cm     [0.8, 1.4]   break@1.325 -> matter_s3_positivity
          scalar_wgc_beta  [0.25,1.0]   break@0.906 -> scalar_wgc
```

So **5 of 11 prefactors are fully slack** and **6 are load-bearing** -- but every load-bearing break is an
**edge effect, never central**: the constructed point stays feasible across **at least 78% of every
prefactor's band** (the worst case), each breaking only in the outer ~10-25% near one end, against a single
named constraint. All 5/5 checks pass.

## What this establishes

A genuine but **marginal** feasible point. The result does NOT rest on most O(1) conventions (the bulk of
every band is fine, and nearly half the prefactors are fully slack), but the constructed point is **not deep
in the interior** either -- it sits close enough to ~6 constraint boundaries that an adverse O(1) excursion
in any one reaches it. That is exactly what one expects of the Chebyshev *center* of a tiny, ~3-dimensional,
non-convex consistent+observed region (v2.333, v2.332): the center of a *small* region is necessarily close
to many of its walls. This also generalizes and confirms v2.344 -- `anomaly_rho` is in the load-bearing
set, breaking at its low edge exactly as predicted (the constructed parity 0.06 exceeds the anomaly budget
near the `rho=0.03` floor). The honest reading: feasibility has ~78%+ headroom in each prefactor
*individually*, not a fat interior cushion.

## Honest scope

This is a **one-at-a-time (OAT)** sweep: each prefactor varies with all others at canonical, so it does NOT
capture JOINT excursions -- several prefactors at their adverse edges together would break feasibility more
easily than any one alone. The per-prefactor headroom (>=78% of each band) is therefore an OPTIMISTIC
upper-bound read of robustness; under joint O(1) variation the point is almost certainly more marginal.
Feasibility is sampled on a 9-point grid, so a narrow infeasible sliver between points could be missed (the
sub-ranges are sampled, not proven). The bands are the engine's own declared factor-of-~2 conventions, not
published numbers, and the stack is the toy-basis encoding with the 4 data constraints (incl. the
cosmic-birefringence hint, v2.329 caveat). "Feasible" is a yes/no on the single constructed point; the
marginality is INFERRED, consistent with the v2.333 ~3D tiny-region picture. Toy basis, O(1) prefactors.
