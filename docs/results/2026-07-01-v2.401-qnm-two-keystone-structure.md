# v2.401 - SWING: the basis has a two-keystone structure (g_4 matter + g_R2 curvature) = matter-gravity locking, with no third hidden degeneracy

## Question

v2.397 counted `g_R2`'s constraint load (26/42) to diagnose the c-a degeneracy. Generalize it: map every
coupling's load -- which are keystone-like (over-determined) vs free, and is there any *other* hidden
degeneracy?

## Result (a keystone pair + a free pair)

```text
  constraint load (# of 42 margins each coupling moves):
    g_4          28  (67%)   KEYSTONE  (matter)
    g_R2         25  (60%)   KEYSTONE  (curvature)
    g_6          17  (40%)
    g_R3         10  (24%)
    g_R2_parity   9  (21%)
    g_8           5  (12%)   free/dark (v2.381)
    g_C           5  (12%)   free (Weyl^2 axis, v2.399)
```

All 5/5 checks pass. The tightness is carried by a **keystone pair**, and the load falls off sharply after it
(67%, 60%, then 40%, ~20%, 12%).

## What this establishes

**The two keystones are exactly the matter-gravity locking made structural.** The matter coupling `g_4` and the
leading curvature coupling `g_R2` are the two most over-determined couplings -- precisely the pair that source,
scale, and cap each other (matter forces `g_R2` via the anomaly, v2.393; matter dominance caps gravity,
v2.389/391). `g_4` is even *more* load-bearing than `g_R2`, consistent with matter dominance and with CMB-S4
being the decisive test of `g_4` (v2.395): the theory's single most-constrained coupling is exactly the one a
near-future experiment will measure. The sharp fall-off after the pair means there is **no third keystone and
no second c-a-type degeneracy**: the c-a resolution correctly landed `g_C` in the low-load / nearly-free bin
(12%, bounded only by the HM wedge), validating v2.399, and the two dark/free couplings (`g_8` matter-top-moment
v2.381; `g_C` Weyl^2 axis v2.399) are exactly the two the theory does not pin. So the whole construction rests on
a two-coupling spine -- the matter+curvature keystone pair -- with everything else derived from them or left
free within a bounded modulus. That is why the feasible region is so small (v2.373) yet has genuine unpinned
directions: it is the structural fingerprint of matter-gravity locking -- two locked keystones, a tail of
derived couplings, two free moduli.

## Honest scope

The load count is a concrete perturb-and-count measure, exact for this stack; a different opt-in set shifts the
exact numbers but not the two-keystone-plus-two-free structure. "Keystone" is basis-dependent (v2.397):
activating `g_C` dropped `g_R2`'s load from 26 to 25 as the Weyl^2 pieces separated, so the count tracks the
basis resolution. "`g_4` is the top keystone" is a robust ordering (28 vs 25; `g_4` appears in matter
positivity, both anomalies, the WGC, BH entropy, species scale, and CMB-S4), but the precise fractions are
stack-specific. "No third degeneracy" is a statement about the CURRENT (post-c-a) basis -- a further resolution
(splitting the matter operators `g_4/g_6/g_8` into independent spin structures, or resolving the `g_R4` rank-3
sub-structure, blocked as unsourceable v2.209) could reveal more, so it means "no MORE degeneracy visible
without a finer basis", not "the basis is complete". A structural map of the constraint set, no new physical
datum; its value is showing WHY the construction is tight (a keystone pair) and confirming `g_C` landed
correctly (low-load, free).
