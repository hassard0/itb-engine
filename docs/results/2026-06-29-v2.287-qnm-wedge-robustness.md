# v2.287 - Is the feasible curvature wedge robust? The realism program applied to v2.286

## Question

The v2.286 wedge (`g_R2 <= ~0.2`, positivity ratio ceiling `x = g_R3/g_R2 ~ 0.83`) was mapped at the
engine's CANONICAL knife-edge prefactors. The repo's whole "honest by construction" ethos is to
distinguish robust conclusions from prefactor artifacts -- so does the wedge survive the documented
O(1)-prefactor uncertainty?

## Result

Sweeping the two dominant positivity prefactors over their documented ranges
(`graviton_fwd_c` in `[0.8, 1.6]`, `efthedron_alpha` in `[0.8, 1.5]`):

```text
  graviton_fwd_c  efthedron_alpha   x_ceiling   g_R2_ceiling
  0.8             0.8               1.20        0.15
  0.8             1.1               1.10        0.15
  0.8             1.5               0.80        0.15
  1.2             1.1 (canonical)   0.80        0.15
  1.6             0.8               0.60        0.15
  1.6             1.5               0.60        0.15

  x-ceiling range [0.60, 1.20];  g_R2-ceiling range [0.15, 0.15]
```

The two walls behave oppositely, and one v2.286 conclusion is honestly demoted:

- **The `g_R2` ceiling is rock-solid robust** -- it stays at `0.15` for EVERY prefactor sample, because
  it is set by anomaly cancellation and the repulsive-force conjecture, not by the tunable positivity
  knobs. "Higher-derivative gravity carries `g_R2` only up to `~0.2`" is a genuinely robust conclusion.
- **The positivity ratio ceiling `x` is strongly prefactor-sensitive**, ranging from `0.60` (tight
  `graviton_fwd_c = 1.6`) to `1.20` (loose `0.8`), and that range **straddles the framework ratios**.
  Since string/AS/cdt sit at `x ~ 0.67-0.75` and lqg at `x = 1.0`, and the ceiling sweeps `0.6 -> 1.2`,
  **which frameworks fail the positivity wall is NOT robust**: at loose prefactors lqg's `x = 1.0` is
  *inside* the wall (it would pass), and at tight prefactors string's `x = 0.75` is *outside* it (it
  would fail). The canonical-prefactor v2.286 picture (lqg out, the others in) is the CANONICAL
  verdict, not a prefactor-robust one.

All 5/5 checks pass (including the check that the verdict is *not* robust).

## What this establishes

The realism program does its job: it confirms the `g_R2` wall as a robust conclusion and demotes the
fine positivity-ratio separation of the frameworks (v2.282/v2.286) to a canonical-only statement, since
it lives within the engine's O(1) prefactor noise. What survives robustly is weaker but real -- the
feasible wedge exists for every prefactor and its `g_R2` wall is fixed; only the individual-framework
positivity verdicts are prefactor-dependent. This is surfaced, not buried.
