# v2.220 - Net R4 reach per overtone: sensitivity x resolvability

## Question

Three cycles built the pieces of one question. v2.217: the n=1 first overtone is ~491x more
sensitive to the R4 quartic operator than the n=0 fundamental (driven by the qEFT damping-time
coefficient `dtq_1 = 171.35`). v2.219: the per-mode ringdown resolvability is `R_f(Q)`,
`R_tau(Q)`. But the overtone is heavily damped -- low quality factor `Q`, hence *worse*
resolvability. **Does the overtone's huge sensitivity survive its resolvability penalty, or does
the penalty cancel the gain?** This cycle multiplies sensitivity x resolvability to find out.

## Method

Per mode `n` and channel, a fractional QNM deviation from coupling `gamma` is `gamma |c_n|`
(`|dwq_n|` for frequency, `|dtq_n|` for damping). It is resolvable at `N` sigma when the
per-mode SNR `rho_n` exceeds `N R(Q_n)/(|c_n| gamma)`, so the reach (smaller = deeper) is

```text
gamma_reach,n(channel) = N * R_channel(Q_n) / (|c_n| * rho_n).
```

At equal SNR this isolates sensitivity x resolvability. `R_f(Q)`, `R_tau(Q)` from v2.219; qEFT
`|dwq_n|`, `|dtq_n|` from v2.217 (source-backed, Silva-Ghosh-Buonanno).

## Result

```text
        Q       R_f     R_tau   reach*rho (freq)   reach*rho (damping)   best channel
n=0   2.100    0.554    2.001       13.10               16.48            frequency  (13.10)
n=1   0.633    3.774    2.529       12.36                0.0738          damping    (0.0738)
```

**The overtone wins decisively -- by ~177x at equal SNR.** Its damping channel reaches
`gamma_reach*rho = 0.074`, ~177x deeper than the fundamental's best (frequency) channel (13.10).
The enormous damping-time sensitivity (`dtq_1 = 171.35`) overwhelms the resolvability penalty:
even though the low `Q = 0.63` balloons `R_f` from 0.55 to 3.77 (the overtone's frequency is
poorly resolved -- correctly reflected), the damping-time channel still dominates by two orders
of magnitude.

**Crossover:** the overtone keeps the deeper reach as long as its SNR exceeds just ~**0.56%** of
the fundamental's (`rho_1/rho_0 > 0.0056`) -- an extremely weak condition, essentially always met
once the overtone is detected at all. Even a heavily SNR-suppressed overtone dominates the R4
constraint.

## What this establishes

The answer is clean: **overtone ringdown is the dominant R4 lever not just in raw sensitivity
(v2.217) but in actual detection reach.** The sensitivity advantage is not an artifact that the
overtone's poor resolvability erases -- it survives the full sensitivity x resolvability product
by ~2 orders of magnitude, concentrated entirely in the damping-time channel. This closes the
arc: overtone sensitivity (v2.217) -> isospectrality discriminator (v2.218) -> resolvability
(v2.219) -> net reach (here), all self-contained and source-backed.

## Honest scope (negatives preserved)

- **Equal-SNR idealization.** The overtone is typically excited at *lower* SNR; reach scales as
  `1/rho_n`, so an excitation ratio `rho_1/rho_0 ~ 0.1-0.3` shrinks the 177x advantage to
  ~18-53x -- still dominant.
- The overtone `Q = 0.63 < 1` is heavily damped, so its single-damped-sinusoid frequency is
  poorly defined (this is *why* `R_f = 3.77`, captured correctly, not a bug).
- White-noise / single-mode / `t=0`-start Fisher (v2.219 caveats).
- qEFT coefficients carry the v2.217 caveats: `dtq_1 = 171.35` makes the overtone perturbatively
  delicate (valid for `gamma << 6e-3`) -- consistent here, since the `~0.07/rho` reach sits deep
  in that regime for `rho >~ 30`.
- Parity-odd `g_R4_c3` stays dark (v2.209); the full isospectrality splitting still needs the
  un-sourceable polar correction (v2.218).
