## The honest caveat (and why SKA, not now)

Following Kato–Soda (2016): the **isotropic monopole** of the circular
polarization **cancels** in an isotropic PTA — a single number can't see net
handedness of a statistically isotropic sky. The chiral signal is therefore
carried by the **anisotropic (dipole and higher) cross-correlations**, accessible
via the kinematic dipole / GWB anisotropy. Current PTAs have just resolved the
*symmetric* HD curve; the **SKA**, with ~1% precision on the correlation *shape*
and the sky coverage to measure anisotropy, is the instrument that could detect a
3–9% chiral component.

circular-polarization degree, not a full anisotropic overlap-function
