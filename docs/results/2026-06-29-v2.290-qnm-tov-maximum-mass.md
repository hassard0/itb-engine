# v2.290 - The neutron-star maximum mass: the TOV limit and the stability turnover

## Question

Builds on v2.289 (TOV machinery, Buchdahl bound). Why can't a neutron star be arbitrarily heavy?
Integrating the Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff equation for a realistic equation of state and sweeping the
central density should reveal whether matter can support unlimited mass.

## Result

For a `Gamma = 2` energy-density polytrope `P = K eps^2` (`K = 220 km^2`, the standard stiff-NS toy),
the integrated mass-radius curve TURNS OVER:

```text
  eps_c (km^-2)   M (Msun)   R (km)    c_s^2(center)
  2.0e-4          0.835      16.94     0.088
  6.3e-4          1.627      14.54     0.278
  1.1e-3          1.897      13.00     0.467
  1.5e-3          1.986      11.93     0.657
  1.9e-3          2.004      11.15     0.846   <- TOV limit (maximum mass)
  2.4e-3          1.993      10.56     1.036   (unstable branch)
  2.8e-3          1.968      10.09     1.225

  M_max = 2.004 Msun at R = 11.15 km
```

The mass rises with central density to a **maximum of 2.00 solar masses at R = 11.2 km** and then
falls -- the TOV limit. The turnover is the stability boundary: below it `dM/d rho_c > 0` (compressing
the star adds mass -- stable, verified), above it `dM/d rho_c < 0` (compressing it LOSES mass -- the
configuration is unstable and collapses to a black hole, verified). This is the relativistic effect
with no Newtonian analog: the pressure that should support the star instead adds to the energy density
that gravitates. The EOS stays causal (`c_s^2 <= 0.85 < 1`) over the entire stable branch. All 6/6
checks pass.

## What this establishes

The same TOV machinery that gave the v2.289 Buchdahl compactness bound here gives the mass bound -- the
deep reason neutron stars have a maximum mass. The toy polytrope's `~2 Msun` limit is in the right
ballpark: the heaviest observed pulsar, PSR J0740+6620 (`2.08 Msun`), pushes against it, the GW170817
merger remnant (collapsed at `~2.2-2.3 Msun`) brackets it from above, and the causal Rhoades-Ruffini
argument (sound speed `<= c`) caps ANY neutron star at `~3.2 Msun`. Together with v2.289, the
relativistic-astrophysics sector now bounds both how compact (`beta < 8/9`) and how heavy
(`M < M_TOV`) cold matter can be before it must become a black hole.

## Honest scope

A first-principles RK4 TOV integration, but with a TOY `Gamma = 2` polytrope (`K = 220 km^2`), the
standard stiff-NS demonstrator -- so the maximum mass `~2 Msun` and radius `~11 km` are REPRESENTATIVE
of a stiff EOS, not a prediction from microphysical nuclear matter (a real EOS gives a specific `M_max`
and M-R curve; `K` was set to land the toy near the observed `~2 Msun`). The existence of the turnover,
the sign flip of `dM/d rho_c` (the stability criterion), and the causality of the chosen polytrope are
exact properties of the integration. The observed PSR J0740+6620 mass, the GW170817 remnant bracket and
the Rhoades-Ruffini `~3.2 Msun` causal bound are cited published values, not re-derived. A classical-GR
/ relativistic-astrophysics result, not an engine constraint refit.
