# v2.227 - Covariance-corrected no-hair detectability: the realistic 220/221 forecast

## Question

v2.226 quantified how R4 violates the 220/221 no-hair consistency and gave a critical SNR using
ISOLATED single-mode resolvability -- flagged as optimistic, since the two modes are measured
jointly with strong covariance (v2.221). What is the REALISTIC critical SNR from the full two-mode
joint Fisher, for the no-hair RATIO observables `ln(omega_221/omega_220)` (frequency) and
`ln(omega_I,221/omega_I,220)` (damping)?

## The ratio subtlety

A ratio observable has a subtlety the v2.226 isolated estimate missed: the two modes' parameter
errors are strongly CORRELATED (corr ~ 0.86, v2.221), and correlated errors partially CANCEL in a
ratio. So covariance has two competing effects -- it inflates each mode's individual uncertainty
(~4-5x, v2.221) but the correlation reduces the ratio variance relative to treating the inflated
uncertainties as independent.

## Result

```text
            rho_crit (1 sigma) per gamma
 channel    full-cov   isolated(v2.226)   optimism   corr. mitigation   (full vs diag)
 frequency   26.3         2.90             9.1x         9%
 damping      0.133       0.0188           7.1x        38%

 damping no-hair channel still dominates the frequency channel by ~197x
```

Two findings:

1. **The v2.226 isolated estimate was optimistic by ~7-9x.** The realistic covariance-corrected
   critical SNR is `rho_crit ~ 26/gamma` (frequency) and `~0.13/gamma` (damping) -- the joint
   two-mode fit inflates the ratio uncertainty well beyond the naive independent-mode estimate,
   exactly as the v2.221 covariance inflation implies.

2. **The mode correlation mitigates the ratio variance.** Because correlated errors cancel in a
   ratio, the full-covariance `rho*sigma` is **38% below** the diagonal (correlation-ignoring)
   value in the damping channel and 9% below in the frequency channel -- a genuine, non-obvious
   benefit of the ratio observable that partially offsets the covariance penalty.

The damping no-hair channel still dominates by ~197x, so the qualitative conclusion of the whole
R4 program (overtone damping is the lever) survives the realistic covariance treatment.

## What this establishes

Closes the v2.226 detectability gap with the realistic two-mode covariance, and surfaces the
ratio-observable correlation benefit. It is the honest, covariance-correct detectability forecast
for the R4 no-hair test: optimistic isolated estimates should be downgraded ~7-9x, but the ratio
structure and the dominant damping channel keep the test viable at modest `rho/gamma`.

## Honest scope

Equal-amplitude, white-noise, `t=0`-start two-mode Fisher (v2.221 idealizations). The R4 violation
magnitudes are source-backed (qEFT `dwq`/`dtq`, v2.217) and the ratio covariance is from first
principles. This REFINES the v2.226 isolated estimate (optimistic by ~7-9x) toward realism without
overturning the qualitative result (damping dominates). A realistic overtone excitation
`rho_1/rho_0 < 1` would raise `rho_crit` further (reach scales as `1/rho_1`). The `dtq_1`
perturbative-delicacy caveat carries (v2.217, `gamma << 6e-3`). Parity-odd `g_R4_c3` stays dark
(v2.209).
